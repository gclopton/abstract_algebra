
Every integer $n \geq 2$ can be written as a product of prime numbers, and this factorization is unique up to the order of the factors (and, in $\mathbb{Z}$, up to multiplication by the unit -1 ). Equivalently, each $n \geq 1$ has a unique prime-power factorization

$$
n=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{\alpha_i} \quad\left(\alpha_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\right)
$$

where the $p_i$ are distinct primes and the exponents $\alpha_i$ are uniquely determined.
