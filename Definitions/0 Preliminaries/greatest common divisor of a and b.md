
For nonzero integers $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}-\{0\}$, there is a unique positive integer $d$, called the greatest common divisor of $a$ and $b$ (or g.c.d. of $a$ and $b$ ), satisfying:
	(a) $d \mid a$ and $d \mid b$ (so $d$ is a common divisor of $a$ and $b$ ), and
	(b) if $e \mid a$ and $e \mid b$, then $e \mid d$ (so $d$ is the greatest such divisor).


**Note:** Equivalently: $d$ is the positive generator of the ideal $\{a x+b y: x, y \in \mathbb{Z}\}$, i.e., Bézout's identity $d=a x_0+b y_0$ holds for some $x_0, y_0 \in \mathbb{Z}$.
