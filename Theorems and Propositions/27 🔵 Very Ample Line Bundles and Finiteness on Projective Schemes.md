


This chapter develops three closely related finiteness results for projective schemes. The starting point is the notion of a very ample invertible sheaf, which gives an embedding into projective space. From there we prove the "global generation for high twists" part of Serre's theorem, then a finiteness theorem for global sections of coherent sheaves on projective schemes over finitely generated algebras, and finally a corollary on the coherence of direct images under projective morphisms.

Throughout, schemes are assumed to be quasi-separated and we freely use standard facts about projective space, Proj of a graded ring, and coherent sheaves.



# 1. Very Ample Line Bundles (Relative Version)

Let $f: X \rightarrow Y$ be a morphism of schemes and let $\mathcal{L}$ be an invertible (line) sheaf on $X$.
We say that $\mathcal{L}$ is very ample relative to $Y$ if there exists an immersion

$$
i: X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}_Y^n
$$

such that $X$ is identified with an open subscheme of a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}_Y^n$, and such that

$$
\mathcal{L} \cong i^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_Y^n}(1)
$$


In this situation it is customary to denote $\mathcal{L}$ by $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$.
There is a more general relative notion where one considers immersions into projective bundles $\mathbb{P}_Y(\mathcal{E})$ for some locally free sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ on $Y$, but we restrict here to the case of relative projective spaces.

When $Y=\operatorname{Spec} A$ is affine, a projective $A$-scheme $X$ is, by definition, a scheme admitting a closed immersion

$$
i: X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}_A^n
$$

for some $n$. If $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is the restriction of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}(1)$ to $X$, then $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is very ample relative to $\operatorname{Spec} A$.

Note that if $X$ is projective over a noetherian ring $A$, then $X$ is proper over $A$. Hence an immersion $X \rightarrow \mathbb{P}_A^n$ is automatically a closed immersion, so in this situation "very ample" really gives a closed embedding into projective space.

For any integer $n \in \mathbb{Z}$, we write

$$
\mathcal{F}(n):=\mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \mathcal{O}_X(1)^{\otimes n}
$$

for the $n$-th twist of a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$.



# 2. Serre's Global Generation Theorem (Theorem 49)

We now state and prove the "global generation for large twists" part of Serre's theorem.
Let $A$ be a noetherian ring, let $X$ be a projective $A$-scheme, and let $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ be a very ample invertible sheaf on $X$ (relative to $\operatorname{Spec} A$ ). Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

> [!theorem]
> Theorem 49 (Serre).
> There exists an integer $n_0$ such that for all $n \geq n_0$, the twisted sheaf $\mathcal{F}(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections. Equivalently, for $n \gg 0$ the canonical morphism
> 
> $$
> \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) \otimes_A \mathcal{O}_X \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}(n)
> $$
> 
> is surjective.




## 2.1 Reduction to Projective Space

Since $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ is very ample relative to $\operatorname{Spec} A$, there exists an immersion

$$
i: X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}_A^n
$$

such that

$$
\mathcal{O}_X(1) \cong i^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}(1)
$$


Because $X$ is projective (hence proper) over $A$, the immersion $i$ is in fact a closed immersion. Closed immersions are finite morphisms, so $i$ is finite.

For any coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module $\mathcal{F}$, the direct image $i_* \mathcal{F}$ is then a coherent $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^{n-}}$-module, because finite morphisms preserve coherence.

The twisting behaves well with respect to the direct image: by the projection formula, for each integer $n$ we have

$$
i_*(\mathcal{F}(n))=i_*\left(\mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} i^* \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}(n)\right) \cong i_* \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}} \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}(n)=\left(i_* \mathcal{F}\right)(n)
$$


Moreover, taking global sections commutes with $i_*$ because $i$ is finite and hence affine:

$$
\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) \cong \Gamma\left(\mathbb{P}_A^n, i_* \mathcal{F}(n)\right)
$$


Therefore, the statement of Theorem 49 for $(X, \mathcal{F})$ is equivalent to the same statement for $\left(\mathbb{P}_A^n, i_* \mathcal{F}\right)$. Hence it suffices to prove the theorem in the special case

$$
X=\mathbb{P}_A^n
$$


From now on in the proof of Theorem 49 we assume $X=\mathbb{P}_A^n, \mathcal{O}_X(1)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}(1)$, and $\mathcal{F}$ is a coherent sheaf on $\mathbb{P}_A^n$.


## 2.2 Local Generators and Extension of Sections

The projective space $\mathbb{P}_A^n$ admits the usual affine open covering by the standard distinguished opens

$$
\mathbb{P}_A^n=\bigcup_{i=0}^n D_{+}\left(x_i\right),
$$

where $x_0, \ldots, x_n$ are the homogeneous coordinates on $\mathbb{P}_A^n=\operatorname{Proj} A\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]$, and $D_{+}\left(x_i\right)$ is the basic open where $x_i$ does not vanish. Each $D_{+}\left(x_i\right)$ is affine.

On each affine open $D_{+}\left(x_i\right)$, the restriction $\left.\mathcal{F}\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}$ is a coherent sheaf on an affine scheme, hence globally generated by finitely many sections on that open. More precisely, for each $i$ one can choose finitely many sections

$$
t_{i j}^{\prime} \in \Gamma\left(D_{+}\left(x_i\right), \mathcal{F}\right)
$$

such that these sections generate $\left.\mathcal{F}\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}$ as an $\mathcal{O}_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}$-module.
At this point we invoke an earlier "extension of sections" result (Theorem 42 in the notes). That theorem states, roughly, the following. Let $X$ be projective over a noetherian ring and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent sheaf. If $s$ is a global section of $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ and $U= D_{+}(s)$ is the corresponding affine open subset, then for any section $\sigma^{\prime} \in \Gamma(U, \mathcal{F})$ there exists an integer $n_0$ such that for all $n \geq n_0$, the product $s^n \sigma^{\prime}$ extends to a global section of $\mathcal{F}(n)$.

In our situation, for each $i$ the coordinate $x_i$ is a global section of $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_A^n}(1)$, and the corresponding distinguished open is precisely $D_{+}\left(x_i\right)$. Thus Theorem 42 applies with $s=x_i$ and $\sigma^{\prime}=t_{i j}^{\prime}$.


Therefore, there exists an integer $n_0$ (independent of $i$ and $j$ ) such that for all $n \geq n_0$, each product

$$
x_i^n t_{i j}^{\prime} \in \Gamma\left(D_{+}\left(x_i\right), \mathcal{F}(n)\right)
$$

extends to a global section

$$
t_{i j} \in \Gamma\left(\mathbb{P}_A^n, \mathcal{F}(n)\right)
$$


For a fixed $n \geq n_0$, consider all these global sections $t_{i j}$ as a finite set of generators. They define a natural morphism

$$
\varphi_n: \mathcal{O}_X^N \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}(n)
$$

where $N$ is the total number of sections $t_{i j}$, and where $\varphi_n$ sends the $j$-th basis vector on the $i$-th index to the corresponding section $t_{i j}$.

By construction, when we restrict $\varphi_n$ to the open set $D_{+}\left(x_i\right)$, the image contains all the elements $x_i^n t_{i j}^{\prime}$, and these generate $\left.\mathcal{F}(n)\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}$ because multiplication by the nowhere-vanishing function $x_i^n$ is an isomorphism $\left.\left.\mathcal{F}\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)} \cong \mathcal{F}(n)\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}$. Thus each restriction

$$
\left.\varphi_n\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}:\left.\mathcal{O}_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}^N \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}(n)\right|_{D_{+}\left(x_i\right)}
$$

is surjective for all $i$, and the opens $D_{+}\left(x_i\right) \operatorname{cover} X$. Hence $\varphi_n$ is surjective globally.
This shows that for all $n \geq n_0$, the sheaf $\mathcal{F}(n)$ is generated by finitely many global sections, completing the proof of Theorem 49.



# 3. Finiteness of Global Section (Theorem 50)

We now turn to a finiteness result for global sections of coherent sheaves on projective schemes over finitely generated algebras.

Let $k$ be a field and let $R$ be a finitely generated $k$-algebra. Let $X$ be a projective $R$ scheme and $\mathcal{F}$ a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

Theorem 50.
The $R$-module $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ is finitely generated. In particular, when $R=k$, the space $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ is finite-dimensional as a $k$-vector space.

There are more general finiteness statements valid for projective schemes over noetherian bases, which can be proved using cohomological methods. Here we follow a more concrete graded approach.

The proof proceeds in two stages. First we treat a special case where $X$ is Proj of a graded domain and $\mathcal{F}$ is a twist of the structure sheaf. Then we reduce the general case to this special case by standard graded-module arguments and a filtration by prime quotients.


# 4. The Special Case: $X=\operatorname{Proj} A, \mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_X(n)$

Assume that $A$ is a finitely generated graded $k$-algebra

$$
A=\bigoplus_{d \geq 0} A_d
$$

which is an integral domain. Set $R=A_0$; in the situation of Theorem 50 we assume that $R$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra. Suppose that the irrelevant ideal

$$
A_{+}=\bigoplus_{d>0} A_d
$$

is generated by finitely many elements $x_0, \ldots, x_r$ in degree 1 :

$$
A_{+}=\left(x_0, \ldots, x_r\right) .
$$


Let $X=\operatorname{Proj} A$, and consider the invertible sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(n)$. We focus first on the sheaves $\mathcal{O}_X(n)$ themselves as our coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$.

A basic observation is that multiplication by any $x_i$ induces an injective map on global sections of these twists. Indeed, for each integer $n$ there is a natural homomorphism

$$
\Gamma\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)\right) \xrightarrow{x_i} \Gamma\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n+1)\right) .
$$


This map is induced by multiplication by the homogeneous element $x_i \in A_1$. The graded ring $A$ is an integral domain, so multiplication by $x_i$ on $A$ is injective. The sections of $\mathcal{O}_X(n)$ can be described in terms of homogeneous elements of degree $n$ in certain localizations of $A$; under this description multiplication by $x_i$ is still injective. Hence each of these maps on global sections is injective.

In particular, to prove that $\Gamma\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)\right)$ is a finitely generated $R$-module for all integers $n$, it suffices to prove this for all sufficiently large $n$. If one degree is finitely generated and every smaller degree injects into it via multiplication by the $x_i$, one can deduce finite generation in all degrees. Thus we are allowed to restrict to the case $n \gg$ 0.


To understand all twists simultaneously, consider the graded $R$-module

$$
\Gamma_*\left(\mathcal{O}_X\right):=\bigoplus_{n \geq 0} \Gamma\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)\right)
$$


For $\operatorname{Proj} A$ there is a standard description of $\Gamma_*\left(\mathcal{O}_X\right)$ in terms of localizations of $A$. Denote by $A_{x_i}$ the localization of $A$ at the multiplicative set $\left\{1, x_i, x_i^2, \ldots\right\}$. Then one has an identification

$$
\Gamma_*\left(\mathcal{O}_X\right) \cong \bigcap_{i=0}^r A_{x_i}
$$

inside the localization $A_{x_0, \ldots, x_r}$ obtained by inverting all the $x_i$. Concretely, this intersection is taken in the fraction field of $A$ (the total ring of fractions if $A$ is only assumed reduced).

For convenience write

$$
\tilde{A}:=\Gamma_*\left(\mathcal{O}_X\right) \subseteq A_{x_0, \ldots, x_r}
$$


The elements of $\tilde{A}$ are precisely those fractions which are regular (i.e. come from degree-wise homogeneous elements) on all standard affine opens $D_{+}\left(x_i\right)$.

The goal is to show that $\tilde{A}$ is integral over $A$. Once this is known, we can conclude the desired finiteness. Indeed, $A$ is a finitely generated algebra over a field, so its integral closure in any finite extension of its fraction field is a finitely generated $A$-module. The ring $\tilde{A}$ sits inside the fraction field of $A$ and is integral over $A$, hence $\tilde{A}$ is a finitely generated $A$-module. Since $\tilde{A}$ is graded, each graded piece $\tilde{A}_n=\Gamma\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)\right)$ is a finitely generated $A_0=R$-module.

Thus the task reduces to showing that each element $\alpha \in \tilde{A}$ is integral over $A$.



# 5. Integrality of $\tilde{A}$ over $A$

Let $\alpha^{\prime} \in \tilde{A}_d$ be a homogeneous element of degree $d$. By construction $\tilde{A} \subseteq A_{x_i}$ for each $i$. Hence for each $i$ there exists an integer $m_i$ such that

$$
x_i^{m_i} \alpha^{\prime} \in A .
$$


Since there are only finitely many $i$, we can choose a single integer $m$ larger than all the $m_i$ so that

$$
x_i^m \alpha^{\prime} \in A \quad \text { for all } i=0, \ldots, r .
$$


Now, the degree- $N$ part $A_N$ of $A$ is generated (as an $R$-module) by monomials in the $x_i$ of total degree $N$. By increasing $m$ if necessary, we can guarantee that for every monomial $y$ of degree $m$ we have

$$
y \alpha^{\prime} \in A .
$$


Intuitively, this is possible because every such monomial $y$ is a product of suitable powers of the $x_i$, and we have already ensured that sufficiently large powers of each $x_i$ kill the denominators of $\alpha^{\prime}$.

In particular, every element of $A_m$ multiplied by $\alpha^{\prime}$ lies in $A$. Since $A_{+}$is generated in degree 1, the higher degree pieces $A_{\geq m}=\bigoplus_{N \geq m} A_N$ are generated by products of elements in $A_m$ with elements of lower degree. Combining this with the previous observation and the homogeneity of $\alpha^{\prime}$, one sees that all products

$$
y \cdot\left(\alpha^{\prime}\right)^q \in A_{\geq m}
$$

for all integers $q \geq 0$ and all homogeneous $y \in A_{\geq m}$.


for all integers $q \geq 0$ and all homogeneous $y \in A_{\geq m}$.
Consider now the $A$-submodule of the fraction field generated by the elements $\left(\alpha^{\prime}\right)^q$ for $0 \leq q \leq Q$ for some large $Q$, together with the degree $\geq m$ part of $A$. Because each product $y\left(\alpha^{\prime}\right)^q$ lands back in a finite sum of graded pieces of $A$ (specifically in degrees $\geq m$ ), one can choose $Q$ large enough so that all higher powers of $\alpha^{\prime}$ are $A$ linear combinations of the first $Q$ powers with coefficients in $A$.

This shows that the $A$-subalgebra $A\left[\alpha^{\prime}\right]$ is contained in a finitely generated $A$ submodule of the fraction field. An element whose powers generate a finitely generated $A$-module in this way is integral over $A$. Hence $\alpha^{\prime}$ is integral over $A$. Since $\alpha^{\prime}$ was arbitrary, every element of $\tilde{A}$ is integral over $A$, and $\tilde{A}$ is integral over $A$.

As explained above, it follows that $\tilde{A}$ is a finitely generated $A$-module, and therefore each graded piece $\tilde{A}_n=\Gamma\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)\right)$ is a finitely generated module over $A_0=R$. This proves Theorem 50 in the special case $X=\operatorname{Proj} A$ and $\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_X(n)$.


# 6. The General Case via Graded Modules and Filtrations

We now return to the general situation of Theorem 50: $X$ is a projective scheme over a finitely generated $k$-algebra $R$, and $\mathcal{F}$ is an arbitrary coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

There are two standard reductions. First, since $X$ is projective over $R$, there exists a closed immersion

$$
X \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}_R^n
$$

for some $n$. Equivalently, there is a graded $R$-algebra

$$
A \cong R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right] / I,
$$

where $I$ is a homogeneous ideal contained in the positive-degree part $\left(R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]\right)_{+}$, such that

$$
X \cong \operatorname{Proj} A .
$$


Second, every coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $\operatorname{Proj} A$ arises (up to isomorphism) from a finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$ by the usual tilde construction:

$$
\mathcal{F} \cong \widetilde{M} .
$$


Moreover, twisting $\mathcal{F}$ corresponds to graded shifts of $M$.
Define the graded $R$-module of twisted global sections

$$
M_*:=\Gamma_*(\mathcal{F}):=\bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F}(n)) .
$$


This $M_*$ has a natural structure of graded $A$-module. In fact, one can arrange that $\mathcal{F} \cong \widetilde{M}$ for some finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$ such that $M$ and $M_*$ are closely related (the precise construction is standard and can be found, for example, in Hartshorne's treatment of coherent sheaves on $\operatorname{Proj} A$ ).

A general fact from graded commutative algebra (see, for instance, Hartshorne, Proposition I.7.4) asserts that there exists a finite filtration of the graded module $M$

$$
0=M^{(0)} \subset M^{(1)} \subset \cdots \subset M^{(\ell)}=M
$$

by graded submodules such that each successive quotient has the form

$$
M^{(i)} / M^{(i-1)} \cong A / \mathfrak{p}_i\left[\ell_i\right]
$$

for some homogeneous prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}_i \subset A$ and some integer $\ell_i$. Here $\left[\ell_i\right]$ denotes the degree shift in the grading.

Passing to associated sheaves, the filtration of $M$ gives a filtration of the coherent sheaf $\widetilde{M} \cong \mathcal{F}:$

$$
0=\widetilde{M^{(0)}} \subset \widetilde{M^{(1)}} \subset \cdots \subset \widetilde{M^{(\ell)}}=\widetilde{M}=\mathcal{F} .
$$


The successive quotients are

$$
\overline{M^{(i)} / M^{(i-1)}} \cong \widehat{A / \mathfrak{p}_i\left[\ell_i\right]} .
$$


If we set $Z_i:=\operatorname{Proj}\left(A / \mathfrak{p}_i\right)$ and let

$$
i_i: Z_i \hookrightarrow X
$$


be the induced closed immersion, then one can identify

$$
\widehat{A / \mathfrak{p}_i\left[\ell_i\right]} \cong\left(i_i\right) \cdot \mathcal{O}_{Z_i}\left(\ell_i\right) .
$$


Thus each quotient sheaf in the filtration of $\mathcal{F}$ is, up to pushforward, a twist of the structure sheaf of some closed subscheme $Z_i$ of $X$ of the form $\operatorname{Proj}\left(A / \mathfrak{p}_i\right)$.

For such a quotient, global sections behave nicely:

$$
\Gamma\left(X, \widehat{A / \mathfrak{p}_i\left[\ell_i\right]}\right) \cong \Gamma\left(Z_i, \mathcal{O}_{Z_i}\left(\ell_i\right)\right)
$$


By the special case already proved, applied to $Z_i=\operatorname{Proj}\left(A / \mathfrak{p}_i\right)$ and $\mathcal{O}_{Z_i}\left(\ell_i\right)$, this is a finitely generated $R$-module.

Now consider the short exact sequences of sheaves coming from the filtration:

$$
0 \longrightarrow \widehat{M^{(i-1)}} \longrightarrow \widehat{M^{(i)}} \longrightarrow \widehat{M^{(i)} / M^{(i-1)}} \longrightarrow 0 .
$$


Taking global sections gives exact sequences of $R$-modules

$$
0 \longrightarrow \Gamma\left(X, \widehat{M^{(i-1)}}\right) \longrightarrow \Gamma\left(X, \widehat{M^{(i)}}\right) \longrightarrow \Gamma\left(X, \widehat{M^{(i)} / M^{(i-1)}}\right),
$$

up to higher cohomology terms which do not affect the finiteness of cokernels in this context. Since the rightmost terms are finitely generated $R$-modules (by the special case), an easy induction on $i$ shows that each $\Gamma\left(X, \widehat{M^{(i)}}\right)$ is a finitely generated $R$ module. In particular,

$$
\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})=\Gamma(X, \widetilde{M})=\Gamma\left(X, \widetilde{M^{(\ell)}}\right)
$$

is a finitely generated $R$-module.

This completes the proof of Theorem 50 in full generality.


# 7. Coherence of Direct Images under Projective Morphisms (Corollary 51)

As an immediate application of Theorem 50 we obtain a standard coherence statement for direct images under projective morphisms.

Let $f: X \rightarrow Y$ be a projective morphism between schemes of finite type over a field $k$, and let $\mathcal{F}$ be a coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module.

Corollary 51.
The direct image $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module.
Proof.
The question of coherence is local on the target, so we may assume that $Y$ is affine:

$$
Y=\operatorname{Spec} R
$$

for some finitely generated $k$-algebra $R$. Because $f$ is projective and of finite type, $X$ is a projective $R$-scheme. The direct image $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is quasi-coherent on $Y$, and on an affine scheme quasi-coherent sheaves correspond to modules. More precisely, there is an $R$ module

$$
M:=\Gamma\left(Y, f_* \mathcal{F}\right) \cong \Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})
$$

such that

$$
f_* \mathcal{F} \cong \widetilde{M}
$$


By Theorem 50, $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ is a finitely generated $R$-module. Thus $M$ is finitely generated, and the associated sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ is a coherent $\mathcal{O}_Y$-module. Hence $f_* \mathcal{F}$ is coherent.

This completes the proof of the corollary.



# 8. Summary

Starting from the notion of a relatively very ample line bundle, which encodes the data of a projective embedding and a distinguished twist $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$, we have proved three fundamental finiteness results for projective schemes:
1. For a projective scheme $X$ over a noetherian ring and a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$, high twists $\mathcal{F}(n)$ are generated by finitely many global sections (Serre's theorem on global generation).
2. For a projective scheme $X$ over a finitely generated $k$-algebra $R$, the global sections $\Gamma(X, \mathcal{F})$ of any coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ form a finitely generated $R$-module.
3. For a projective morphism $f: X \rightarrow Y$ between schemes of finite type over a field, the direct image sheaf $f_* \mathcal{F}$ of a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent.

Together, these results underpin much of the algebraic geometry of projective schemes: they provide the bridge from geometric data (coherent sheaves on projective varieties) back to algebraic data (finitely generated modules over finitely generated algebras), which is essential for both structural theory and concrete computations.
