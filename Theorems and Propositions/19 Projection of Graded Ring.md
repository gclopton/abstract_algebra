
Let $A=\oplus_{n \geq 0} A_n$ be a graded ring.
A ideal $I \subset A$ is homogeneous iff $I=\oplus_n\left(A_n \cap I\right) \Longleftrightarrow I$ is generated by homogeneous elts.
Write $A_{+}=\bigoplus_{n \geq 1} A_n$.



> [!definition] $\operatorname{Proj}(A)$ ("relevant prime ideals")
> 
> $$
> \operatorname{Proj}(A)=\left\{\mathfrak{p} \mid \mathfrak{p} \subset A \text { homogeneous prime s.t. } A_{+} \nsubseteq \mathfrak{p}\right\} .
> $$
> 




> [!definition] Topology on $\operatorname{Proj}(A)$
> For a homogeneous ideal $I \subset A$ let
> 
> $$
> V_{+}(I)=\{\mathrm{p} \in \operatorname{Proj}(A) \mid I \subseteq \mathrm{p}\} .
> $$
> 
> 
> The closed subsets of $\operatorname{Proj}(A)$ are these. Check that this defines a topology:
> 
> $$
> V_{+}(I J)=V_{+}(I) \cup V_{+}(J), \quad V_{+}\left(\sum I_i\right)=\bigcap V_{+}\left(I_i\right) .
> $$
> 


> [!NOTE]
> $\operatorname{Proj}(A) \subseteq \operatorname{Spec}(A)$, and for an ideal $I \subset A$,
> 
> $$
> V(I) \cap \operatorname{Proj}(A)=V_{+}\left(I^h\right) \quad\left(I^h=\text { homogeneous ideal generated by } I\right) .
> $$
> 
> $\Rightarrow \operatorname{Proj}(A) \subseteq \operatorname{Spec}(A)$ has the subspace topology.



Given $f \in A_n$ (a homogeneous elt), set

$$
D_{+}(f)=\{p \in \operatorname{Proj}(A) \mid f \notin p\} \subset \operatorname{Proj}(A) .
$$


This is open because

$$
D_{+}(f)=\operatorname{Proj}(A) \backslash V_{+}((f)) .
$$


> [!NOTE]
> note: $D_{+}(f)=\operatorname{Proj}(A) \cap D(f) \leftarrow$ subset of $\operatorname{Spec} A$.



# Graded localization and relevant primes

The localization $A_f$ is graded when $f$ is homogeneous:

$$
\operatorname{deg}\left(\frac{a}{f^n}\right)=\operatorname{deg}(a)-n \cdot \operatorname{deg}(f) .
$$


Write $A_{(f)} \subseteq A_f$ for the degree- 0 elements (i.e.

$$
A_{(f)}=\left\{\left.\frac{a}{f^n} \right\rvert\, \operatorname{deg}(a)=n \cdot \operatorname{deg}(f)\right\} .
$$


If $I \subset A$ is homogeneous, write $I_{+}=I \cap A_{+}$.
(margin note) prime $\Rightarrow \mathbf{p}$ is relevant $\Leftrightarrow \mathbf{p}_{+} \neq A_{+}$.


> [!proposition] Proposition 27 - Basic facts for a graded ring
> 
> Let $A$ be a graded ring.
> 1. Let $\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{p}^{\prime} \subset A$ be relevant primes. If $\mathbf{p}_{+}=\mathbf{p}_{+}^{\prime}$, then $\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}^{\prime}$.
> 2. A homogeneous ideal $I \subset A_{+}$is of the form $\mathbf{p}_{+}$for some relevant prime $\mathbf{p}$
> 
> $$
> \Longleftrightarrow \quad a b \notin I \text { for any } a, b \in A_{+} \backslash I .
> $$
> 
> 3. Let $S \subset A$ be a multiplicative set which contains an element of $A_{+}$. The set of homogeneous $I \subsetneq A_{+}$with $S \cap I \neq \varnothing$ has maximal elements, and each is of the form $\mathfrak{p}_{+}$ for some relevant prime.
> 4. Let $I \subset A_{+}$be a homogeneous ideal. Then
> 
> $$
> \operatorname{rad}(I)_{+}=\operatorname{rad}(I) \cap A_{+} \text {is homogeneous, }
> $$
> 
> and
> 
> $$
> \operatorname{rad}(I)_{+}=A_{+} \cap \bigcap_{\substack{I \subseteq p \\ \text { p relevant prime }}} \mathfrak{p}=\bigcap_{\substack{I \subseteq p \\ \text { prelevant prime }}} \mathfrak{p}_{+} .
> $$





**Proof (sketch):**
Let $I \subsetneq A_{+}$be homogeneous, $f \in A_{+} \backslash I$. Then if $I \subseteq \mathfrak{p}_{+}$(prime), set

$$
\mathfrak{p}_0=\left\{a \in A_0 \mid f^r \cdot a \in I_{r \operatorname{deg}(f)} \forall r \geq 1\right\} \quad \text { (exercise: why?) }
$$

(1) immediate, follows from 1.
(2) Check that $\mathrm{p}_0 \oplus I$ is prime.
(3) Zorn's lemma $\Rightarrow \exists$ maximal elements. Check that maximal elements satisfy the condition in (2).
(4) Show

$$
\operatorname{rad}(I)=\bigcap_{\substack{I \subseteq \mathrm{p} \\ \text { relevant prime }}} \mathfrak{p} .
$$


Suffices to consider $I=0$ (replace $A$ by $A / I$ ).
$\subseteq \checkmark$
If $f \notin \operatorname{rad}(I)$ then a max'l element in the set

$$
\left\{I \subsetneq A_{+} \mid I \cap\left\{1, f, f^2, \ldots\right\}=\varnothing\right\}
$$

is a relevant prime. Thus $f \notin \bigcap_{\substack{I \subset p \\ \text { relevant }}}$
p.
v. $\square$



> [!proposition] Proposition 28 - Induced homeomorphism $D_{+}(f) \rightarrow \operatorname{Spec}\left(A_{(f)}\right)$
> 
> The homeomorphism $D(f) \cong \operatorname{Spec}\left(A_f\right)$ induces a homeomorphism
> 
> $$
> \psi_f: D_{+}(f) \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Spec}\left(A_{(f)}\right), \quad \mathfrak{p} \longmapsto \mathfrak{p} A_f \cap A_{(f)} .
> $$
> 

**Proof:** We first define a map going the other direction.
If $\mathrm{q} \subset A_{(f)}$ is a prime ideal, set

$$
q_n=\left\{a \in A_{n \operatorname{deg}(f)} \mid a / f^n \in q\right\} \quad(n \geq 0) .
$$


Define

$$
\Theta_f(q)=\bigoplus_n q_n .
$$


This is a set map $\Theta_f: \operatorname{Spec}\left(A_{(f)}\right) \rightarrow D_{+}(f)$ which is inverse to $\psi_f$ (by Prop. 27(1)). Hence $\psi_f$ is bjective.

To see it is a homeomorphism, observe: if $I$ is a homogeneous ideal, then

$$
I \subset \mathfrak{p} \Longleftrightarrow I A_f \cap A_{(f)} \subset \mathfrak{p} A_f \cap A_{(f)} \quad \text { (exercise: check this), }
$$

and the underbraced term is $I_{(f)}$. Therefore

$$
\psi_f(V(I))=V\left(I_{(f)}\right)
$$

so $\psi_f$ is a closed mapping.



# Sheaf on $X=\operatorname{Proj}(A)$ and projective space
Let $X=\operatorname{Proj}(A)$ and define a sheaf of rings on $X$ :
- The subsets $D_{+}(f)$ for $f \in A_{+}$homogeneous form a basis for the topology.
- Define

$$
\Gamma\left(D_{+}(f), \mathcal{O}_X\right):=A_{(f)}
$$

- For $D_{+}(g) \subset D_{+}(f)$ the restriction maps are given by

$$
A_{(f)} \rightarrow\left(A_{(f)}\right)_{g(f)}=A_{(g)} \quad \text { where } \quad g_{(f)}=\frac{g^{\operatorname{deg} f}}{f^{\operatorname{deg} g}}
$$


Immediate that $\left(X, \mathcal{O}_X\right)$ is a scheme.
Example. Let $R$ be a ring and put
$\operatorname{Proj}\left(R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]\right)=: \mathbf{P}_R^n$, where
$R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]$ is the polynomial ring graded by $\left|x_i\right|=1$.
Then $\mathbf{P}_R^n$ has the open covering by

$V_i:=D_{+}\left(x_i\right) \cong \operatorname{Spec}\left(R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]\left(x_i\right)\right) \cong \operatorname{Spec}\left(R\left[\frac{x_0}{x_i}, \ldots, \frac{\widehat{x_i}}{x_i}, \ldots, \frac{x_n}{x_i}\right]\right) \cong \mathbf{A}_R^n$.

Thus this agrees with our previous definition of $\mathbf{P}^{\boldsymbol{n}}$ (verify the gluings are the same).

Observe also: since

$$
\mathbf{A}_R^n \cong \mathbf{A}_Z^n \times{ }_{\operatorname{Spec} Z} \operatorname{Spec}(R),
$$

we also have

$$
\mathbf{P}_R^n \cong \mathbf{P}_Z^n \times_{\operatorname{Spec} Z} \operatorname{Spec}(R) .
$$

$$

# Functoriality 

$\operatorname{Proj}(A)$ is unchanged by "thinning $A$ out" or "changing $A_0$ "

Given a graded ring $A=\oplus_{i \geq 0} A_i$.
Precisely: let $k, d \geq 1$ and define a new graded ring $A^{\prime}$ by

$$
A_0^{\prime}=A_0, \quad A_i^{\prime}=0(0<i<k), \quad A_i^{\prime}=A_{d i}(i \geq k) .
$$


If $f \in A_{+}$, then $f^{k d} \in A_{+}^{\prime}$, and we have

$$
D_{+}(f)=D_{+}\left(f^{k d}\right) \quad \text { as well as } \quad A_{(f)} \cong A_{\left(f^{k d}\right)}^{\prime} .
$$


We thus find an isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Proj}(A) \cong \operatorname{Proj}\left(A^{\prime}\right)
$$



> [!warning]
> Induced maps on Proj need not exist on all of Proj $(B)$


If $\varphi: A \rightarrow B$ is a graded ring map, it can happen that $\varphi^{-1}(p)$ fails to be relevant when $p$ is relevant.
$\Rightarrow$ we do not always have an induced map

$$
\operatorname{Proj}(B) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Proj}(A) .
$$


Let

$$
G(\varphi)=\left\{q \in \operatorname{Proj}(B) \mid \varphi^{-1}(q) \neq A_{+}\right\} \subset \operatorname{Proj}(B)
$$

(an open subset), equivalently

$$
G(\varphi)=\bigcup_{\substack{f \in A_{+} \\ \text {homogeneous }}} D_{+}(\varphi(f))
$$


Then we do have a well-defined map $G(\varphi) \rightarrow \operatorname{Proj}(A)$.
In particular, if $\varphi: A \rightarrow B$ is surjective then $G(\varphi)=\operatorname{Proj}(B)$, and we have

$$
\operatorname{Proj}(B) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Proj}(A)
$$

which is a closed immersion; it induces an isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Proj}(B) \cong \operatorname{Proj}(A / \operatorname{ker}(\varphi)) .
$$
