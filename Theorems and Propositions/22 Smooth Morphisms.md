

> [!definition] Smooth Morphism
> Let $f: X \rightarrow Y$ be a morphism and $d \geq 0$.
> - Say that $f$ is smooth of relative dimension $d$ at $x \in X$ if there exist affine open neighbourhoods $U \subset X$ of $x$ and $V \subset Y$ of $f(x)$, and an open immersion
> 
> $$
> j: U \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Spec} R\left[T_1, \ldots, T_n\right] /\left(f_1, \ldots, f_{n-d}\right)
> $$
> 
> of $R$-schemes such that the Jacobian
> 
> $$
> J_{f_1, \ldots, f_{n-d}}(x)=\left(\frac{\partial f_f}{\partial T_j}(x)\right)_{i, j} \quad[\text { an }(n-d) \times n \text { matrix with entries in } k(x)]
> $$
> 
> has rank $n-d$.
> - Say that $f$ is smooth if it is smooth at all points of $X$. (relative dim $d$ )





> [!proposition] Proposition 32
> If $f$ is smooth of relative dimension $d$ at $x \in X$, then there is an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$ such that $f$ is smooth of relative dimension $d$ at each point of $U$.
> 
> In particular, the smooth locus of $f$,
> 
> $$
> \{x \in X \mid f \text { smooth at } x\} \subset X,
> $$
> 
> is open.



**Proof:** Immediate from the definition
(rank condition is equivalent to saying there exists a non-vanishing minor of the Jacobian which does not vanish at $x$ )




> [!proposition] Proposition 33
> (1) The property of $f: X \rightarrow Y$ being smooth is local on both source and target.
> (i.e. $f$ is smooth at $x \in X \Longleftrightarrow$ the restriction $\left.f\right|_U: U \rightarrow Y$ is smooth for an open nbhd $U$ of $x \Longleftrightarrow f^{-1}(V) \rightarrow V$ is smooth where $V$ is an open nbhd of $f(x)$.)
> (2) If $f: X \rightarrow Y$ is smooth of relative dimension $d$ at $x \in X, Y^{\prime} \rightarrow Y$ any map, and $z \in X \times_Y Y^{\prime}$ a point which projects to $x$, then
> 
> $$
> f^{\prime}: X \times_Y Y^{\prime} \longrightarrow Y^{\prime}
> $$
> 
> is smooth of relative dimension $d$ at $z$.
> (3) Open immersions are smooth of relative dimension 0 .



**proof:** straightforward.



### Examples:

(1) $\mathrm{A}_S^n \rightarrow S$ is smooth of relative dimension $n$ over $S$. $\mathbb{P}_S^n \rightarrow S$ is smooth (same).
(2) $X \subset \mathbb{A}_k^2$ ( $k$ alg. closed field) defined by $y^2=f(x)$ and $f$ has no multiple zeros (in Spec $k[x, y]$ ). Then the Jacobian has maximal rank at all points $\Rightarrow$ smooth.

We'll see: If $X$ a $k$-scheme (locally noetherian), $x \in X$ and $k(x)=k$, then $X$ smooth at $x \Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x}=\operatorname{dim} T_x X$.



# 23 Regular Schemes



Recall. A noetherian local ring ( $R, \mathrm{~m}$ ) is regular if

$$
\operatorname{dim}_{R / \mathrm{m}}\left(\mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{m}^2\right)=\operatorname{dim} R .
$$

(So $(R, \mathrm{~m})$ is regular $\Longleftrightarrow \mathrm{m}$ is generated by $\operatorname{dim} R$ elements, since $\operatorname{dim} R \leq \operatorname{dim}_{k(\mathrm{~m})}\left(\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{m}^2\right)$ in general.)

> [!definition] regular locally noetherian scheme
> A locally noetherian scheme is regular at $x \in X$ if $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is a regular local ring. $X$ is regular if it is regular at all $x \in X$.



> [!example]



- $\quad \mathbf{A}_k^n, \mathbb{P}_k^n$ are regular at all points. ( $k$ a field.)
- Let $C \subset \mathbb{A}_k^2$ be a curve defined by $y^2=x^3$.

At any point other than $(0,0), C$ is regular.


> e.g. note that the coord. ring can be identified with $k\left[z^2, z^3\right] \subset k[z]$ and localization at any prime other than $(z) \subset k[z]$ induces an isomorphism
> 
> $$
> k\left[z^2, z^3\right]_p \cong k[z]_p .
> $$
> 
> 
> But at ( 0,0 ): the max ideal of $k\left[z^2, z^3\right]_{\left(z^2, z^3\right)}$ equals ( $z^2, z^3$ ) and is generated by 2 elts! 
> 



$(0,0)$ is a singular point.)


> [!proposition] Proposition 34
> Let $X$ be locally of finite type over a field $k$. Suppose $X$ is smooth of relative dim. $d$ at $x \in X$. Then $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is regular and $\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq d$. If $x$ is closed then $\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x}=d$.



**proof:** First assume that $x$ is closed. From the definition of smoothness, it follows that

$$
\mathcal{O}_{X, x} \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}_k^n, y} /\left(f_1, \ldots, f_{n-d}\right)
$$

where $f_i \in k\left[T_1, \ldots, T_n\right]$ and $J_{f_1, \ldots, f_{n-d}}(y)$ has rank $n-d$ (emb ed a nbd of $x$ into $\mathbb{A}^n, y=$ image of $x$ ).


**Commutative Algebra**

If $(R, \mathrm{~m})$ is a regular local ring and $a_1, \ldots, a_i$ such that the images are linearly independent in $\mathrm{m} / \mathrm{m}^2$, then

$$
R /\left(a_1, \ldots, a_i\right) \text { is a regular local ring of } \operatorname{dim} \operatorname{dim} R-i .
$$


Now if $k=k(y)$ then $\left\{\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial T_i}\right|_y\right\}$ forms a basis of $\left(\mathrm{m}_y / \mathrm{m}_y^2\right)^{\mathrm{V}} \cong T_y \mathrm{~A}_k^n$.
Hence
$\left\{\left[f_1\right]_y, \ldots,\left[f_{n-d}\right]_y\right\}$ is linearly independent in $\mathrm{m}_y / \mathrm{m}_y^2$ (rank Jacobian $=n-d$ ).

In general $k \subset k(y)$ finite extension. Let $y^{\prime} \in X \times_{\text {Spec } k} \operatorname{Spec}(k(y))$ be a point mapping to $y$; $\mathrm{m}_{y^{\prime}}=k(y)\left[T_1, \ldots, T_n\right]_{\mathrm{m}_{y^{\prime}}}$ is the max. ideal.
Consider

$$
\mathrm{m}_y / \mathrm{m}_y^2 \longrightarrow \mathrm{~m}_{y^{\prime}} / \mathrm{m}_{y^{\prime}}^2
$$

a $k(y)$-linear map (via $\left\{\left[T_1\right], \ldots,\left[T_n\right]\right\}$ ). But note

$$
J_{f_1, \ldots, f_{n-d}}(y)=J_{f_1, \ldots, f_{n-d}}\left(y^{\prime}\right),
$$

so by the previous observation $\left\{\left[f_1\right], \ldots,\left[f_{n-d}\right]\right\}$ is lin. ind. in $\mathrm{m}_{y^{\prime}} / \mathrm{m}_{y^{\prime}}^2 \Rightarrow$ lin. ind. in $\mathrm{m}_y / \mathrm{m}_y^2$.


Now if $x$ is not closed, let $U$ be an open nbd of $x$ such that $U$ is smooth. Then $U$ has a point $x^{\prime} \in U$ which is closed in $X$. `(*)`
Then $\mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is a localization of $\mathcal{O}_{X, x^{\prime}}$, which we've seen is regular.
$\Rightarrow \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is also regular. $\square$

---


$$
\left(\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \leq \operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x^{\prime}} \leq d \text { since } \mathcal{O}_{X, x} \text { is a localization of } \mathcal{O}_{X, x^{\prime}}\right)
$$


Remark. If $X$ is a locally finite type $k$-scheme (field $k$ ), then

$$
x \in X \text { is closed } \Longleftrightarrow k \subset k(x) \text { is a finite extension. }
$$

" $\Rightarrow$ " follows from the Nullstellensatz (applied to an affine nbd Spec $\boldsymbol{A}$ of $\boldsymbol{X}$ ).
" $\Leftarrow$ ": Let $\mathrm{p} \in A$ correspond to $x$. Then

$$
k \subset A \longrightarrow A_{\mathrm{p}} \subset k(x)=\operatorname{Frac}(A / \mathrm{p}) .
$$


Every element is a root of a monic polynomial with coefficients in $k$; so given $\alpha \in A_p$, the map

$$
k[\alpha] \longrightarrow k(x)
$$

is injective ( $A_{\mathrm{p}}$ a domain), hence $\alpha$ is a unit. Therefore $A_{\mathrm{p}}$ is a field, so $x$ is closed in $\operatorname{Spec}(A) \subset X$ (for any affine nbd of $x$ ). Hence $x$ is closed in $X$.
`(*)` This remark shows that if $x \in U \subset X$ open is closed in $U$, it is closed in $X$.



> [!proposition] Proposition 35
> Let $k$ be a field and $X=V\left(g_1, \ldots, g_s\right) \subset \mathbb{A}_k^n$.
> Suppose $x \in X$ is a closed point and
> 
> $$
> \operatorname{rank}_x J_{g_1, \ldots, g_s}(x)=n-\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x}(=d)
> $$
> 
> 
> Then $X$ is smooth of relative dimension $d$ at $x$.



**Proof:** Reorder the $g_i$ so that $J_{g_1, \ldots, g_{n-d}}(x)$ has full rank.
Then $Y:=V\left(g_1, \ldots, g_{n-d}\right) \subset \mathbb{A}_k^n$ is smooth and we have $x \in X \subset Y$.
We show this is true in a nbd of $x$. It suffices to work locally: we show

$$
\mathcal{O}_{Y, x} \xrightarrow{\varphi} \mathcal{O}_{X, x} .
$$


> Later we'll show (in a reasonably large generality) such an iso is flat; so an alt. exercise: argue directly using that both of these are domains (to be seen), so are subrings of a common fraction field.



By Prop. 34, $\mathcal{O}_{Y, x}$ is regular of dimension $d$.
But then $\varphi: \mathcal{O}_{Y, x} \rightarrow \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is surjective between $d$-dimensional rings.
Since $\mathcal{O}_{Y, x}$ is a domain $\Rightarrow \varphi$ is an iso [Exercise].
(Equivalently: Spec $\mathcal{O}_{Y, x}$ is reduced \& irreducible of $\operatorname{dim} d_i \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_{X, x}$ is closed of $\operatorname{dim} d \Rightarrow$ they have to agree.) $\square$

> [!corollary] Corollary 36
> Let $X$ be a locally finite-type $k$-scheme, $x \in X$ with $k(x)=k$. Then
> 
> $$
> X \text { is smooth of rel. } \operatorname{dim} d \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{dim}_k T_x X=\operatorname{dim} \mathcal{O}_{X, x}=d .
> $$
> 
> 
> Proof. " $\Rightarrow$ " follows from Prop. 34.
> " $\Leftarrow$ " Can assume $X$ affine, $X=V\left(g_1, \ldots, g_s\right) \subset \mathbb{A}_k^n$ some $n$.
> Since $T_x X=\operatorname{ker}\left(J_{g_1, \ldots, g_*}(x)\right) \subseteq T_{A_k^n, x} \cong k^n$, this follows from Prop. 35.
