15\. Checking Properties Affine-Locally
=======================================

Schemes are assembled by gluing affine pieces. To use this effectively, one wants to define and verify geometric properties “on an affine cover” without ambiguity coming from different choices of cover. The key technical tools are (1) a common principal neighborhood on overlaps of affine opens and (2) a gluing/communication lemma for properties that behave well under localization and finite principal covers. This chapter develops these tools and records the standard “local on the target” viewpoint for morphisms.

Throughout, all schemes are Zariski, and an **affine open** means an open subscheme isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec} A$ for some ring $A$. For an open subscheme $U$ of a scheme $X$ and a section $f\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)$, the **distinguished open**

$$D_U(f)=\{\,x\in U : f_x\in \mathcal O_{U,x}\text{ is a unit}\,\}$$

is an open subset of $U$. If $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $f\in A$, then $D_U(f)\cong\operatorname{Spec}(A_f)$, and the sets $D_U(f)$ form a basis for the topology on $U$; intersections satisfy $D_U(f)\cap D_U(g)=D_U(fg)$. Affine schemes $\operatorname{Spec}A$ are quasicompact, and more precisely: a family $\{D(f_i)\}_{i\in I}$ covers $\operatorname{Spec}A$ if and only if the ideal $(f_i)_{i\in I}$ is the unit ideal; equivalently, some finite subfamily already covers.

Common principal neighborhoods on overlaps
------------------------------------------

> \[!lemma\] **Lemma 17 (Common distinguished neighborhood).**  
> Let $X$ be a scheme and $U,V\subset X$ affine opens. For any $x\in U\cap V$, there exists an open subscheme $W\subset U\cap V$ that is simultaneously a distinguished affine open in $U$ and in $V$.

**Proof.** Because the $D_V(g)$ form a basis of the topology on $V$, there is a section $g\in\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_V)$ with $x\in D_V(g)\subset U\cap V$. Set $W:=D_V(g)$. Then $W$ is distinguished in $V$ by construction. Restricting $g$ to $U$ gives $g|_U\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)$, and by the functoriality of the construction of principal opens one has

$$D_V(g)\cap U \;=\; D_U(g|_U).$$

Since $D_V(g)\subset U$, it follows that $W=D_U(g|_U)$ is also a distinguished open in $U$. Finally, $W\subset U\cap V$ and $x\in W$ by construction. $\square$

Communication of properties across affine opens
-----------------------------------------------

We now formalize the precise hypothesis under which a property of affine open subschemes propagates from one affine cover to every affine open.

> \[!lemma\] **Lemma 18 (Affine Communication).**  
> Let $\Phi$ be a property of affine open subschemes of a fixed scheme $X$ such that:
> 
> (i) (Stability under localization) If $\operatorname{Spec}(A)\subset_{\mathrm{open}} X$ has $\Phi$, then for every $f\in A$, the distinguished open $\operatorname{Spec}(A_f)\subset X$ has $\Phi$.
> 
> (ii) (Finite principal gluing) If $f_1,\dots,f_n\in A$ generate the unit ideal and each $\operatorname{Spec}(A_{f_i})\subset X$ has $\Phi$, then $\operatorname{Spec}(A)\subset X$ has $\Phi$.
> 
> Suppose $X=\bigcup_i \operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ is an affine cover with each $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)\subset X$ having $\Phi$. Then every affine open $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\subset X$ has $\Phi$.

**Proof.** Fix $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\subset X$. For each $x\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)$, choose an index $i$ with $x\in \operatorname{Spec}(B)\cap \operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$. Apply Lemma 17 to the pair $U=\operatorname{Spec}(B)$, $V=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ to find a common principal neighborhood

$$x\in W_x=D_B(g_x)=D_{A_i}(g_x')\ \subset\ \operatorname{Spec}(B)\cap \operatorname{Spec}(A_i),$$

where $g_x\in B$ and $g_x'$ is its image in $A_i$ under restriction. The family $\{W_x\}_{x\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)}$ is an open cover of $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ by distinguished opens $D_B(g_x)$. Since $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ is quasicompact and distinguished opens form a basis, there exist finitely many points $x_1,\dots,x_m$ such that

$$\operatorname{Spec}(B)=\bigcup_{j=1}^m D_B(g_{x_j}) \qquad\text{with}\qquad D_B(g_{x_j})=D_{A_{i_j}}(g_{x_j}').$$

By hypothesis each $\operatorname{Spec}(A_{i_j})$ has $\Phi$, so by (i) each $D_{A_{i_j}}(g_{x_j}')$ has $\Phi$. Identifying $D_{A_{i_j}}(g_{x_j}')$ with $D_B(g_{x_j})$, we conclude that each $D_B(g_{x_j})$ has $\Phi$. Because the $g_{x_j}$ define a finite principal cover of $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ with $(g_{x_1},\dots,g_{x_m})=B$, condition (ii) implies $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ has $\Phi$. $\square$

> \[!definition\] **Affine-local property.**  
> A property $\Phi$ of affine open subschemes of $X$ is **affine-local (on $X$)** if it satisfies (i) and (ii) in Lemma 18.

In particular, for an affine-local $\Phi$ the assignment “$\operatorname{Spec}(U)\subset X$ has $\Phi$” is independent of the chosen affine cover used to check it: if one affine cover satisfies $\Phi$ piecewise, then every affine open does.

The morphism viewpoint: “local on the target”
---------------------------------------------

The affine-local formalism naturally applies to properties framed for morphisms. Let $\Phi$ be a property of schemes that makes sense for open subschemes (e.g. “reduced,” “regular,” etc.). For a morphism $f:X\to Y$, we will say that **$f$ has $\Phi$ (on the target)** if for every affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A\subset Y$ the open subscheme $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\subset X$ has $\Phi$. When $\Phi$ is affine-local, Lemma 18 shows that this condition can be checked on any single affine cover of $Y$.

Formally:

> \[!proposition\] **Local on the target (affine form).**  
> Let $\Phi$ be affine-local on every scheme. For a morphism $f:X\to Y$, the following are equivalent.  
> (a) For every affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A\subset Y$, the scheme $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ has $\Phi$.  
> (b) There exists an affine open cover $Y=\bigcup_i \operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ such that each $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}(A_i))$ has $\Phi$.

**Proof.** The implication (a)$\Rightarrow$(b) is immediate. For (b)$\Rightarrow$(a), fix $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\subset Y$. The open immersion $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}(B))\hookrightarrow X$ is covered by the affine opens $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}(B)\cap \operatorname{Spec}(A_i))$. By Lemma 17 applied inside $Y$, each $\operatorname{Spec}(B)\cap \operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ can be covered by opens that are simultaneously distinguished in $\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ and $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$. Pulling back along $f$ preserves distinguished opens and finite covers. The hypotheses (i)–(ii) for $\Phi$ on $X$ then apply exactly as in the proof of Lemma 18 to show $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}(B))$ has $\Phi$. $\square$

This is the standard “local on the target” principle you will use repeatedly: to show $f$ has $\Phi$, it suffices to check $\Phi$ on the preimages of a single affine cover of $Y$.

Examples and non-examples
-------------------------

It is useful to see the hypotheses (i)–(ii) at work in concrete situations.

**Example (Reduced is affine-local).** Let $\Phi(U)$ be “$U$ is reduced.” If $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is reduced, then so is $\operatorname{Spec}(A_f)$ for every $f\in A$ because localization does not create nilpotents; thus (i) holds. For (ii), suppose $1\in(f_1,\dots,f_n)\subset A$ and each $A_{f_i}$ is reduced. If $a\in A$ is nilpotent, then its image in each $A_{f_i}$ is $0$, so for each $i$ there exists $m_i$ with $f_i^{m_i}a=0$. Let $M=\max m_i$; then $f_i^M a=0$ for all $i$. Choose $u_1,\dots,u_n\in A$ with $\sum_i u_i f_i^M=1$; multiplying by $a$ gives $a=\sum_i u_i(f_i^M a)=0$. Hence $A$ is reduced. By Lemma 18, reducedness can be checked on any affine cover.

**Non-example (Being a domain is not affine-local).** Take $A=k\times k$. Then $\operatorname{Spec}A$ is the disjoint union of two copies of $\operatorname{Spec}k$. Let $e_1=(1,0)$, $e_2=(0,1)$. The principal opens $D(e_1)$ and $D(e_2)$ cover $\operatorname{Spec}A$ and correspond to $\operatorname{Spec}k$, which are domains. But $A$ itself is not a domain. Condition (ii) fails for the property “is a domain,” so Lemma 18 does not apply.

**Remark (Other affine-local properties).** Many familiar scheme-level conditions are affine-local—including being regular, Cohen–Macaulay, Gorenstein, $S_n$ or $R_m$ in Serre’s sense, normal, and reduced—because they are preserved under localization and detectable on finite principal covers. In later chapters, these will also be phrased as properties of morphisms that are “local on the source and target” in the Zariski topology.

### Appendix: background facts used above

For completeness, we record the two standard facts invoked implicitly.

**Distinguished opens form a basis.** If $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $W\subset U$ is open with $x\in W$ corresponding to a prime $\mathfrak p\subset A$, choose $f\in A\setminus\mathfrak p$ whose vanishing locus avoids a neighborhood of $x$ disjoint from $U\setminus W$. Then $x\in D(f)\subset W$. Intersections satisfy $D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg)$.

**Quasicompactness of $\operatorname{Spec}A$.** If $\operatorname{Spec}A=\bigcup_{i\in I} D(f_i)$, then the closed set $V((f_i)_{i\in I})$ is empty, hence $\sqrt{(f_i)}=A$, so $1=\sum_{j=1}^m a_j f_{i_j}^{N}$ for some finite subfamily and integer $N$. Thus $\operatorname{Spec}A=\bigcup_{j=1}^m D(f_{i_j})$.

16.1. Some finiteness properties

### Locally noetherian and noetherian schemes

**Definition.** A scheme $X$ is **locally noetherian** if there exists a cover $X=\bigcup \operatorname{Spec} A_i$ with each $A_i$ a noetherian ring. The scheme $X$ is **noetherian** if it is locally noetherian and quasi-compact.

**Note.** By quasicompactness of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ and the basis of distinguished opens, $X$ is noetherian if and only if there exists a _finite_ affine cover $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^n \operatorname{Spec} A_i$ with each $A_i$ noetherian.

**Proposition 19 (Characterization of locally noetherian).**  
A scheme $X$ is locally noetherian if and only if for every affine open $\operatorname{Spec} R\subseteq X$, the ring $R$ is noetherian.

**Proof.** It suffices to verify that the assertion “$\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$ is noetherian” is affine-local (so Lemma 18 applies).

(i) If $U=\operatorname{Spec}R\subset X$ is open and $R$ is noetherian, then every localization $R_f$ is noetherian for $f\in R$.

(ii) Suppose $U=\operatorname{Spec}R$ is covered by finitely many principal opens $D(f_i)$ with each $\operatorname{Spec}(R_{f_i})$ noetherian. Let $I\subset R$ be an ideal. For each $i$, the extension

$$J_i:=IR_{f_i}\subset R_{f_i}$$

is finitely generated. Choose generators of $J_i$ in the form

$$\frac{x_{ij}}{f_i^{\,n_{ij}}}\qquad(j=1,\dots,r_i)$$

with $x_{ij}\in I$. Let $M\subset R$ be the ideal generated by all the finitely many $x_{ij}$. Then $M_{f_i}=I_{f_i}$ for every $i$. Consequently, for every prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$,

$$(I/M)_{\mathfrak p}\;\cong\;(I/M)\otimes_R R_{\mathfrak p}\;=\;0,$$

so $I/M=0$. Hence $I=M$ is finitely generated, and $R$ is noetherian. $\square$

### Finiteness conditions for ring maps and morphisms

**Recall (finite / finitely generated / finitely presented $A$\-algebras).**  
For a ring map $f:A\to B$:

*   $f$ is **finite** if $B$ is a finite (i.e. finitely generated) $A$\-module.
    
*   $f$ is **of finite type** if $B\cong A[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I$ for some ideal $I$.
    
*   $f$ is **finitely presented** if $B\cong A[x_1,\dots,x_n]/I$ with $I$ _finitely generated_.
    

**Definition (finite morphism).**  
A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **finite** if for every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset S$, the preimage $f^{-1}(U)$ is affine and the induced map

$$\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_S)\longrightarrow \Gamma\!\bigl(f^{-1}(U),\mathcal O_X\bigr)$$

is finite (i.e. the target is a finite module over the source).

**Definition (locally of finite type / locally finitely presented).**  
A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **locally of finite type** if for any affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset S$ and any affine $V\subset f^{-1}(U)$, the ring map

$$\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_S)\longrightarrow \Gamma(V,\mathcal O_X)$$

is of finite type.  
It is **locally finitely presented** if, in the same situation, that ring map is finitely presented.

**Example (a finite map by squaring).**  
Consider $\mathbb A^1_{\mathbf C}\to\mathbb A^1_{\mathbf C}$ induced by

$$\mathbf C[u]\longrightarrow \mathbf C[t],\qquad u\longmapsto t^2.$$

As a $\mathbf C[u]$\-module, $\mathbf C[t]$ is generated by $1,t$; hence the map is finite.

**Note.** From the definition alone it is not immediate that a _finite ring map_ $A\to B$ induces a _finite morphism_ $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$. We will shortly state that “finite” is affine-local on the base; this implies in particular that a finite ring map indeed gives a finite morphism of schemes.

Over $\mathbf C$, the map $t\mapsto t^2$ folds $\mathbb A^1_{\mathbf C}$ over itself with a single branch point at $0$: an “X-shaped” double cover of the line ramified at $0$. (Exercise: justify this picture using the behavior over $D(u)$ and over the fiber at $u=0$.)

### Affine-locality for finite type / finite presentation of $A$\-algebras

**Proposition 20 (Affine-locality for $A$\-algebras).**  
Let $A$ be a commutative ring. The properties “$B$ is a finite-type $A$\-algebra” and “$B$ is a finitely presented $A$\-algebra” are affine-local on $\operatorname{Spec}B$. Precisely:

*   (i) If $B$ is of finite type (resp. finitely presented) over $A$, then so is $B_g$ for any $g\in B$.
    
*   (ii) If $g_1,\dots,g_n\in B$ generate the unit ideal and each $B_{g_i}$ is of finite type (resp. finitely presented) over $A$, then $B$ is of finite type (resp. finitely presented) over $A$.
    

**Proof (finite type case; the finitely presented case is omitted here).**  
(i) If $x_1,\dots,x_r$ generate $B$ as an $A$\-algebra, then $x_1,\dots,x_r,\hat g$ generate $B_g$ as an $A$\-algebra, where $\hat g$ denotes the image of $g^{-1}\in B_g$.

(ii) Write $1=\sum_i b_i g_i$ with $b_i\in B$. For each $i$, choose a finite set $\{x_{ij}/g_i^{\,m}\}$ of $A$\-algebra generators of $B_{g_i}$ (after clearing denominators, we may take a common exponent $m$). Let

$$R:=A\langle\, b_i g_i,\; c_{ij}\,\rangle\ \subset\ B,$$

where $c_{ij}\in B$ are numerators with $x_{ij}=c_{ij}/g_i^{\,m}$. Then $R_{g_i}=B_{g_i}$ for all $i$. If $\mathfrak p\subset B$ is prime, some $g_i\notin\mathfrak p$ (because $(g_1,\dots,g_n)=B$), hence $(B/R)_{\mathfrak p}\cong (B/R)_{g_i}=0$. Therefore $B/R=0$, so $B=R$ is generated over $A$ by the finitely many $b_i g_i$ and $c_{ij}$. $\square$

### Affine-locality on the base for finiteness conditions

**Proposition 21 (Affine-locality on $S$).**  
Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism. Each of the following properties is affine-local on $S$:

*   **Finite.** $f$ is finite if and only if there exists an affine open cover $S=\bigcup \operatorname{Spec}A_i$ such that for each $i$,
    
    $$f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_i)\ \text{is affine, say } \operatorname{Spec}B_i,$$
    
    and $B_i$ is a finite $A_i$\-module. **(Proof postponed.)**
    
*   **Locally of finite type / locally finitely presented.**  
    $f$ is locally of finite type (resp. locally finitely presented) if and only if there exists an affine open cover $S=\bigcup\operatorname{Spec}A_i$ such that each preimage $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_i)$ admits an affine open cover by spectra of finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A_i$\-algebras.
    

**Proof.** For the “only if” direction there is nothing to show. For the converse in the finite-type / finitely-presented cases, we verify the two clauses (i)–(ii) of affine-locality on $S$ and then apply Lemma 18.

(i) Let $\operatorname{Spec}A\subset S$ be affine and suppose $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\to \operatorname{Spec}A$ is locally of finite type (resp. finitely presented). For any $g\in A$,

$$f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_g)\;=\;f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\cap f^{-1}(D(g))$$

is open in $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$. If $\operatorname{Spec}B\subset f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_g)$ is affine, then $\operatorname{Spec}B$ is also an affine open of $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$, so $B$ is a finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A$\-algebra. Let $b\in B$ be the image of $g$. Then $B_b$ is a finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A_g$\-algebra by Proposition 20(i). Thus $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_g)\to \operatorname{Spec}A_g$ is locally of finite type (resp. finitely presented).

(ii) Suppose $g_1,\dots,g_n\in A$ generate the unit ideal and each

$$f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i})\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i}$$

is locally of finite type (resp. finitely presented). Let $\operatorname{Spec}B\subset f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ be affine. Put $b_i\in B$ for the image of $g_i$. Then $(b_1,\dots,b_n)=B$ and

$$\operatorname{Spec}(B_{b_i})\;=\;f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i})\cap \operatorname{Spec}B.$$

By assumption, each $B_{b_i}$ is a finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A_{g_i}$\-algebra. Since every localization $A_{g_i}$ is itself a finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A$\-algebra, Proposition 20(ii) implies that $B$ is a finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A$\-algebra. Hence $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\to \operatorname{Spec}A$ is locally of finite type (resp. finitely presented). $\square$

16.2. Quasi-compact and finite-type morphisms
---------------------------------------------

**Definition (quasi-compact morphism).**  
A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **quasi-compact** if there exists an open covering $S=\bigcup \operatorname{Spec}A_i$ such that each preimage $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_i)$ is quasi-compact.

**Exercise.** Show equivalently: $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V\subset S$.

**Definition (finite-type morphism).**  
A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **of finite type** if it is locally of finite type and quasi-compact.

**Examples.**  
(1) If $S=\operatorname{Spec}R$, then $f:X\to S$ is quasi-compact if and only if $X$ is quasi-compact.  
(2) Any closed immersion is quasi-compact and locally of finite type; hence every closed immersion is of finite type.

**Note (finitely presented morphisms).**  
A **finitely presented** morphism $X\to S$ is _not_ merely “locally finitely presented + quasi-compact”; an additional condition will be introduced later.

### Brief roadmap of usage

The pattern now is standard. For a geometric property phrased in terms of coordinate rings on affine pieces (e.g. “finite type,” “finitely presented,” and—after the postponed proof—“finite”), the property is stable under localization and glues across finite principal covers. Lemma 18 then promotes checks made on one convenient affine cover of the base to _all_ affine opens, eliminating any dependence on the chosen cover.

**Heuristic for the picture $t\mapsto t^2$.**

24.1. $\mathcal O_X$\-modules and basic constructions
-----------------------------------------------------

An **$\mathcal O_X$\-module** $\mathcal F$ is a sheaf of abelian groups on $X$ such that for every open $U\subset X$ the group $\mathcal F(U)$ is an $\mathcal O_X(U)$\-module and the restriction maps $\mathcal F(V)\to\mathcal F(U)$ are $\mathcal O_X(V)$\-linear via $\mathcal O_X(V)\to\mathcal O_X(U)$. A **morphism** of $\mathcal O_X$\-modules is a morphism of sheaves that is $\mathcal O_X(U)$\-linear on sections for every open $U$.

If $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ is a morphism of $\mathcal O_X$\-modules, then

$$\ker(f)(U)=\ker\bigl(\mathcal F(U)\to\mathcal G(U)\bigr),\qquad \operatorname{coker}(f)=\text{ sheafification of }(U\mapsto \operatorname{coker}(\mathcal F(U)\to\mathcal G(U))).$$

On stalks we have

$$\ker(f)_x=\ker(f_x),\qquad \operatorname{coker}(f)_x=\operatorname{coker}(f_x).$$

For $\mathcal O_X$\-modules $\mathcal F,\mathcal F'$, the presheaf

$$U\longmapsto \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_U}\bigl(\mathcal F|_U,\mathcal F'|_U\bigr)$$

is a sheaf; we denote it by $\mathcal{H}\!om_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal F,\mathcal F')$. Its global sections are the $\mathcal O_X$\-linear maps $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal F,\mathcal F')$, and this set is an $\mathcal O_X(X)$\-module by $(af+bg)_U=a|_U\,f_U+b|_U\,g_U$.

Arbitrary products $\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal F_i$ exist and are computed sectionwise. Arbitrary direct sums are the sheafifications of $U\mapsto \bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal F_i(U)$; we denote $\bigoplus_I\mathcal F_i$ also by $\mathcal F^{(I)}$. The **tensor product** $\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal G$ is the sheafification of $U\mapsto \mathcal F(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X(U)}\mathcal G(U)$.

Finally, the canonical map

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal O_X,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow \Gamma(X,\mathcal F),\qquad w\longmapsto w_X(1),$$

is an isomorphism.

### Locally free, invertible, and ideal sheaves

An $\mathcal O_X$\-module $\mathcal F$ is **locally free** if for every $x\in X$ there is an open neighbourhood $U\ni x$ and a set $I$ such that $\mathcal F|_U\simeq \mathcal O_X|_U^{(I)}$. The **rank** of $\mathcal F$ at $x$, written $\operatorname{rk}_x(\mathcal F)$, is the rank of the free $\mathcal O_{X,x}$\-module $\mathcal F_x$.

**Exercise (completed).** The rank function $x\mapsto \operatorname{rk}_x(\mathcal F)$ is locally constant on $X$.  
_Proof._ Fix $x\in X$. Choose $U\ni x$ with $\mathcal F|_U\simeq \mathcal O_X|_U^{(I)}$. For any $y\in U$,

$$\mathcal F_y\simeq (\mathcal O_X^{(I)})_y\simeq \mathcal O_{X,y}^{(I)},$$

so $\operatorname{rk}_y(\mathcal F)$ equals the (fixed) cardinality of $I$. Thus the rank is constant on $U$. $\square$

An **invertible sheaf** is a locally free sheaf of rank $1$. A **sheaf of ideals** $\mathcal I\subset\mathcal O_X$ is an $\mathcal O_X$\-submodule such that $\mathcal I(U)\subset\mathcal O_X(U)$ is an ideal for every open $U$.

24.2. Direct and inverse images; pullback, and an adjunction
------------------------------------------------------------

Let $f:(X,\mathcal O_X)\to (Y,\mathcal O_Y)$ be a morphism of locally ringed spaces with structural map $f^\#:\mathcal O_Y\to f_*\mathcal O_X$.

For an $\mathcal O_X$\-module $\mathcal F$, the **direct image** $f_*\mathcal F$ is an $f_*\mathcal O_X$\-module; via $f^\#$ it acquires a natural $\mathcal O_Y$\-module structure, which we keep implicit. For an $\mathcal O_Y$\-module $\mathcal G$, the inverse image $f^{-1}\mathcal G$ is an $f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y$\-module, and the map $f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_X$ induces the **pullback**

$$f^*\mathcal G:=f^{-1}\mathcal G\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y}\mathcal O_X,$$

an $\mathcal O_X$\-module.

**Proposition 36 (Adjunction).** There is a natural bijection

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}\!\bigl(f^*\mathcal G,\mathcal F\bigr)\ \cong\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Y}\!\bigl(\mathcal G,f_*\mathcal F\bigr).$$

_Proof._ Given $\varphi:f^*\mathcal G=f^{-1}\mathcal G\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y}\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal F$, precompose with the canonical map $f^{-1}\mathcal G\to f^{-1}\mathcal G\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y}\mathcal O_X$ to obtain an $f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y$\-linear map $\widehat\varphi:f^{-1}\mathcal G\to\mathcal F$ (where $\mathcal F$ is viewed as an $f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y$\-module via $f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal O_X$). By the usual adjunction of $f^{-1}\dashv f_*$ for sheaves, $\widehat\varphi$ corresponds uniquely to an $\mathcal O_Y$\-linear map $\varphi^\flat:\mathcal G\to f_*\mathcal F$.

Conversely, given $\psi:\mathcal G\to f_*\mathcal F$, adjunction yields an $f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y$\-linear $\widehat\psi:f^{-1}\mathcal G\to\mathcal F$. By the universal property of the tensor product, there is a unique $\mathcal O_X$\-linear $\psi^\sharp:f^*\mathcal G\to\mathcal F$ with $\psi^\sharp\circ(\text{can})=\widehat\psi$, where “can” is the canonical map $f^{-1}\mathcal G\to f^*\mathcal G$. The two constructions are inverse to one another and are functorial in $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$. $\square$

24.3. The tilde functor on an affine scheme
-------------------------------------------

Let $A$ be a ring, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, and $M$ an $A$\-module. Define a presheaf on the basis of distinguished opens by

$$\widetilde{M}(D(f)):=M_f\qquad (f\in A),$$

with the evident restriction maps $M_f\to M_g$ when $D(g)\subset D(f)$ (i.e. when $g\in (f)$). These restriction maps are localization maps and satisfy the presheaf compatibilities. Because the $D(f)$ form a basis, this determines a unique sheaf $\widetilde{}M$ on $X$; its sections on arbitrary opens are obtained by sheafification. On $D(f)$ there is a natural $A_f$\-module structure on $M_f$, so the assignment $A_f\times M_f\to M_f$ equips $\widetilde{}M$ with an $\mathcal O_X$\-module structure.

An $A$\-linear map $\psi:M\to N$ induces $\widetilde\psi:\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{N}$ by localization on the basis and sheafification; thus

$$(\,\widetilde{\cdot}\,):\operatorname{Mod}_A\longrightarrow \operatorname{Mod}_{\mathcal O_X}$$

is a functor. For $x\in X$ corresponding to a prime $\mathfrak p\subset A$,

$$(\widetilde{M})_x \cong \varinjlim_{x\in D(f)} \widetilde{M}(D(f)) \cong \varinjlim_{f\notin \mathfrak p} M_f \cong M_{\mathfrak p}.$$

**Proposition 37.** For $A$\-modules $M,N$, the maps

$$\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,N)\ \underset{\Gamma}{\stackrel{\ (\widetilde{\phantom{M}})\ }{\rightleftarrows}}\ \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_X}(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{N})$$

are mutually inverse bijections.

_Proof._ First note $\Gamma(X,\widetilde{M})=M$ by construction, hence $\Gamma(\widetilde\psi)=\psi$ for any $A$\-linear $\psi:M\to N$. Thus $(\,\widetilde{\cdot}\,)$ is injective on Hom-sets. For surjectivity, let $w:\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{N}$ be $\mathcal O_X$\-linear, and set $\phi:=\Gamma(w):M\to N$. For each $f\in A$ we have a commutative diagram

$$↓       ↓Mf→ wD(f) Nf\begin{array}{c} M \xrightarrow{\ \phi\ } N\\[2pt] \big\downarrow \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \big\downarrow\\[-2pt] M_f \xrightarrow{\ w_{D(f)}\ } N_f \end{array}$$

so $\phi_f=w_{D(f)}$. Hence $\widetilde{\phi}=w$ on the basis and therefore globally. $\square$

**Corollary 38.** The functor $(\,\widetilde{\cdot}\,):\operatorname{Mod}_A\to\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathcal O_X}$ is fully faithful.

_Proof._ Immediate from Proposition 37. $\square$

**Proposition 39.** Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$.

(1) A sequence $M\to N\to P$ of $A$\-modules is exact if and only if the sequence $\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{N}\to\widetilde{P}$ is exact.

(2) For any $A$\-linear $f:M\to N$,

$$\ker(\widetilde f)\cong \widetilde{\ker(f)},\qquad \operatorname{coker}(\widetilde f)\cong \widetilde{\operatorname{coker}(f)},\qquad \operatorname{im}(\widetilde f)\cong \widetilde{\operatorname{im}(f)}.$$

(3) For a family $\{M_i\}_{i\in I}$,

$$\bigoplus_{i\in I}\widetilde{M_i}\ \cong\ \widetilde{\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i},\qquad \widetilde{M}\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\widetilde{N}\ \cong\ \widetilde{M\otimes_A N}.$$

(4) If $M=\varinjlim_i M_i$ is a filtered colimit, then $\widetilde{M}\cong\varinjlim_i \widetilde{M_i}$.

(5) If $f:\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ corresponds to a ring map $A\to B$, then for any $A$\-module $M$ and any $B$\-module $N$ (viewed as an $A$\-module via restriction of scalars),

$$f^*(\overline{M})\ \cong\ \overline{M\otimes_A B},\qquad f_*(\widetilde{N})\ \cong\ \overline{\,{}_A N\,}.$$

_Proof._  
(1) Exactness of sheaves is local on the basis $\{D(f)\}$. On $D(f)$ we have

$$\Gamma\bigl(D(f),\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{N}\to\widetilde{P}\bigr)\ =\ M_f\to N_f\to P_f,$$

and localization is exact; conversely, if $M\to N\to P$ is exact, then so is the localized sequence on every $D(f)$, hence the sheaf sequence is exact.

(2) Apply (1) to the standard exact sequences defining kernel, image, cokernel in $\operatorname{Mod}_A$ and in $\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathcal O_X}$.

(3) For direct sums, on $D(f)$ we have

$$\Gamma\!\left(D(f),\bigoplus_i\widetilde{M_i}\right)=\bigoplus_i (M_i)_f =\left(\bigoplus_i M_i\right)_f =\Gamma\!\left(D(f),\widetilde{\bigoplus_i M_i}\right).$$

Sheafification respects these identifications. For tensor products, on $D(f)$,

$$\Gamma\!\left(D(f),\widetilde{M}\otimes\widetilde{N}\right) = M_f\otimes_{A_f}N_f \cong (M\otimes_A N)_f =\Gamma\!\left(D(f),\widetilde{M\otimes_A N}\right),$$

using compatibility of localization with tensor products.

(4) On $D(f)$,

$$\Gamma\!\left(D(f),\varinjlim_i \widetilde{M_i}\right)=\varinjlim_i (M_i)_f \cong \left(\varinjlim_i M_i\right)_f =\Gamma\!\left(D(f),\widetilde{\varinjlim_i M_i}\right),$$

since localization commutes with filtered colimits.

(5) For pullback, on the basis $D(a)\subset \operatorname{Spec}A$ with $f^{-1}(D(a))=\bigcup D(b_j)\subset \operatorname{Spec}B$, both sides restrict to $M_a\otimes_{A_a}B_{b_j}$ on each $D(b_j)$, giving a canonical identification that glues. For pushforward, for any $D(a)\subset\operatorname{Spec}A$,

$$\Gamma\!\bigl(D(a),f_*\widetilde{N}\bigr)=\Gamma\!\bigl(f^{-1}D(a),\widetilde{N}\bigr)=\Gamma\!\bigl(\operatorname{Spec}B_a,\widetilde{N}\bigr)=N_a,$$

which agrees with $\Gamma\!\bigl(D(a),\overline{{}_A N}\bigr)$. These identifications are compatible with restriction, hence define the claimed isomorphisms. $\square$

24.4. Quasi-coherent and coherent sheaves
-----------------------------------------

**Definition (quasi-coherent; coherent).** Let $(X,\mathcal O_X)$ be a ringed space. An $\mathcal O_X$\-module $\mathcal F$ is **quasi-coherent** if for every $x\in X$ there exists an open neighbourhood $U\ni x$ and an exact sequence of $\mathcal O_X|_U$\-modules

$$\mathcal O_X|_U^{(J)}\longrightarrow \mathcal O_X|_U^{(I)}\longrightarrow \mathcal F|_U\longrightarrow 0$$

for some sets $I,J$. If $X$ is a noetherian scheme, then $\mathcal F$ is **coherent** if it is quasi-coherent and the sets $I,J$ can be chosen finite.

**Note.** If $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $M$ is an $A$\-module, then $\overline{M}=\widetilde{M}$ is quasi-coherent by construction (indeed, it admits a local presentation by free sheaves obtained from any presentation of $M$).

**Theorem 40.** For a scheme $X$ and an $\mathcal O_X$\-module $\mathcal F$, the following are equivalent:

(i) For every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$ there exists an $A$\-module $M$ with $\mathcal F|_U\simeq \widetilde{M}$.

(ii) There exists an affine open cover $X=\bigcup_i U_i$ with $U_i=\operatorname{Spec}A_i$ and $\mathcal F|_{U_i}\simeq \overline{M_i}$ for some $A_i$\-modules $M_i$.

(iii) $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent.

(iv) For every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$ and every $f\in A$, the natural map

$$\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)_f\ \xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\ \Gamma(D(f),\mathcal F)$$

is an isomorphism.

Moreover, if $X$ is noetherian, the following are equivalent:

(i′) As in (i), with $M$ finitely generated.

(ii′) As in (ii), with each $M_i$ finitely generated.

(iii′) $\mathcal F$ is coherent.

_Proof._  
$(i)\Rightarrow(ii)$ is immediate.

$(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)$. On each $U_i$ the sheaf $\overline{M_i}$ has a presentation by free sheaves induced from any presentation of $M_i$. This is local by construction, so $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent.

$(iii)\Rightarrow(iv)$. Fix an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$. Choose a finite cover $U=\bigcup_j D(g_j)$ and a presentation on $\bigcup_j D(g_j)$

$$\bigoplus_J \widetilde{A_{g_j}}\xrightarrow{\ \phi\ }\bigoplus_I \widetilde{A_{g_j}}\longrightarrow \mathcal F|_{\cup D(g_j)}\longrightarrow 0.$$

By Proposition 37, $\phi=\widetilde{u}$ for some $A$\-linear map $u:\bigoplus_J A_{g_j}\to\bigoplus_I A_{g_j}$. Thus for each $j$,

$$\mathcal F|_{D(g_j)}\ \cong\ \operatorname{coker}(\widetilde{u})|_{D(g_j)}\ \cong\ \overline{\operatorname{coker}(u)}|_{D(g_j)}.$$

Sheaves of the form $\widetilde{}M$ satisfy the localization property $(*)$: for all $f\in A$, $\Gamma(D(f),\widetilde{M})\cong \Gamma(U,\widetilde{M})_f$. Therefore each $\mathcal F|_{D(g_j)}$ satisfies $(*)$, and similarly on overlaps $D(g_jg_{j'})$. Consider the diagram (with products over the finite index sets)

$$0   ↓≅↓≅↓≅0→Γ(D(f),F)→∏jΓ(D(fgj),F)→∏j,j′Γ(D(fgjgj′),F),\begin{aligned} 0&\to \Gamma(U,\mathcal F)_f \to \prod_j \Gamma(D(g_j),\mathcal F)_f \to \prod_{j,j'} \Gamma(D(g_jg_{j'}),\mathcal F)_f\\ &\ \ \ \downarrow\cong\qquad\qquad\downarrow\cong\qquad\qquad\downarrow\cong\\ 0&\to \Gamma(D(f),\mathcal F) \to \prod_j \Gamma(D(fg_j),\mathcal F) \to \prod_{j,j'} \Gamma(D(fg_jg_{j'}),\mathcal F), \end{aligned}$$

where the top row is exact because localization is exact and the sheaf condition is exact on Čech complexes for this finite cover; the vertical arrows are isomorphisms by $(*)$ on each piece. A diagram chase yields $\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)_f\cong \Gamma(D(f),\mathcal F)$.

$(iv)\Rightarrow(i)$. Let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$ be affine and set $M:=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$. For each $f\in A$, hypothesis (iv) identifies $\Gamma(D(f),\mathcal F)$ with $M_f$. These identifications are compatible with restrictions $D(g)\subset D(f)$. Hence $\mathcal F|_U$ and $\widetilde{}M$ agree on the basis $\{D(f)\}$ and glue to an isomorphism $\mathcal F|_U\cong \widetilde{M}$.

For the noetherian part: $(i′)\Rightarrow(ii′)\Rightarrow(iii′)$ is clear from the definitions. For $(iii′)\Rightarrow(ii′)$, reduce to the affine case $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$. By coherence there is an exact sequence with $I,J$ finite

$$\mathcal O_U^{\oplus J}\longrightarrow \mathcal O_U^{\oplus I}\longrightarrow \mathcal F|_U\longrightarrow 0.$$

Taking global sections and using Proposition 39(1) identifies this with

$$A^{\oplus J}\longrightarrow A^{\oplus I}\longrightarrow \Gamma(U,\mathcal F)\longrightarrow 0,$$

so $M:=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$ is a finitely generated $A$\-module and $\mathcal F|_U\cong \overline{M}$ by the first part of the theorem. $\square$

24.5. Affine case: equivalences of categories
---------------------------------------------

**Corollary 41.** Let $A$ be a ring and $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Then the functors

$$(\,\widetilde{\cdot}\,):\operatorname{Mod}_A\ \rightleftarrows\ \operatorname{Qcoh}_{\mathcal O_X}:\Gamma$$

are quasi-inverse equivalences (here $\operatorname{Qcoh}_{\mathcal O_X}$ denotes the category of quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$\-modules). If $A$ is noetherian, then the restrictions

$$(\,\widetilde{\cdot}\,):\operatorname{Mod}_A^{\mathrm{fg}}\ \rightleftarrows\ \operatorname{Coh}_{\mathcal O_X}:\Gamma$$

are also quasi-inverse equivalences (here $\operatorname{Coh}_{\mathcal O_X}$ denotes coherent $\mathcal O_X$\-modules).

_Proof._ For $M\in\operatorname{Mod}_A$, $\Gamma(\widetilde{M})=M$ by construction. For $\mathcal F\in\operatorname{Qcoh}_{\mathcal O_X}$, Theorem 40(iv)$\Rightarrow$(i) (with $U=X$) gives $\mathcal F\cong \widetilde{\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)}$. Naturality is immediate, so the functors are quasi-inverse. In the noetherian case, Theorem 40’s last part identifies coherent sheaves with $\widetilde{}M$ for $M$ finitely generated, and the same argument applies. $\square$

### Summary

On an affine scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$\-modules are exactly the sheaves $\widetilde{}M$ coming from $A$\-modules, and this correspondence is functorially an equivalence with $\Gamma$ as quasi-inverse. Quasi-coherent sheaves on an arbitrary scheme are precisely those that are locally of this form, or, equivalently, those satisfying the localization criterion $\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)_f\cong\Gamma(D(f),\mathcal F)$ on every affine open $U$. Over a noetherian scheme, coherence corresponds to finite generation of the underlying modules on affine patches.

1\. Quasi-separated schemes and morphisms
-----------------------------------------

**Definition.** A scheme $X$ is **quasi-separated** if for any two affine open subsets $U,V\subset X$, the intersection $U\cap V$ is quasi-compact. A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **quasi-separated** if $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-separated for every affine open $V\subseteq Y$.

### Examples

1.  **Separated $\Rightarrow$ quasi-separated (over a base $S$).**  
    Suppose $f:X\to S$ is separated. If $S=\operatorname{Spec}R$ and $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$, $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ are affine opens of $X$, then
    
    $$U\cap V \;=\; \Delta^{-1}(U\times_S V), \qquad U\times_S V\cong \operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R B).$$
    
    The diagonal $\Delta:X\hookrightarrow X\times_S X$ is a closed immersion because $X/S$ is separated; hence $U\cap V$ is closed in the affine $U\times_S V$, therefore affine, hence quasi-compact. Thus $f$ is quasi-separated over $S$. $\square$
    
2.  **Affine schemes are quasi-separated.**  
    If $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is affine and $U,V\subset X$ are affine opens, then (in an affine scheme) affine opens are principal, so $U=D(f)$, $V=D(g)$ for some $f,g\in A$. Thus $U\cap V=D(f)\cap D(g)=D(fg)$, which is affine and hence quasi-compact.
    
3.  **Locally noetherian schemes are quasi-separated.**  
    If $X$ is locally noetherian and $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X$ is affine, then $A$ is noetherian and the topological space $U$ is noetherian; in a noetherian space every open subset is quasi-compact. Since $U\cap V$ is an open subset of the noetherian space $U$, it is quasi-compact.
    

2\. Invertibility loci of sections of line bundles
--------------------------------------------------

Let $\mathcal L$ be an invertible $\mathcal O_X$\-module and $s\in \Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$.

**Definition.** The section $s$ is **invertible at $x\in X$** if there exists an open neighborhood $x\in U\subset X$ and an isomorphism $w:\mathcal L|_U\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O_X|_U$ such that the image of $s_x$ under $w_x:\mathcal L_x\to \mathcal O_{X,x}$ is a unit in $\mathcal O_{X,x}$.

This does not depend on the choice of $U$ or $w$: if $w,w'$ are two local trivializations, then $w'_x\circ w_x^{-1}$ is multiplication by a unit of $\mathcal O_{X,x}$, hence preserves the property of being a unit.

It is useful to record the following equivalent characterizations at a point $x$:

$$s\text{ invertible at }x \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \overline{s}_x\neq 0\text{ in }\mathcal L_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}} k(x) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; s_x\notin \mathfrak m_x\,\mathcal L_x.$$

Indeed, under a local trivialization $w$ near $x$, the image of $s_x$ is an element $a\in \mathcal O_{X,x}$. The three conditions above are respectively: $a\in \mathcal O_{X,x}^\times$; the image of $a$ in $k(x)$ is nonzero; $a\notin \mathfrak m_x$. These are equivalent.

Define the **invertibility locus**

$$X_s\;=\;\{x\in X\mid s\text{ is invertible at }x\}.$$

This is an open subset of $X$. In fact, over any open $U$ with trivialization $w:\mathcal L|_U\simeq \mathcal O_X|_U$, if we set $a:=w(s)|_U\in \Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)$, then

$$X_s\cap U \;=\; U_a,$$

the principal open where $a$ is invertible; hence $X_s$ is open.

3\. Extending sections of quasi-coherent modules (Theorem 42)
-------------------------------------------------------------

**Theorem 42 (Extending sections on a qcqs scheme).**  
Let $X$ be a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme, $\mathcal L$ an invertible $\mathcal O_X$\-module, and $s\in \Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$ a global section. Let $\mathcal F$ be a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$\-module.

1.  If $t\in \Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$ restricts to $0$ on $X_s$, then there exists $n>0$ such that
    
    $$t\otimes s^{\otimes n}=0\ \in\ \Gamma\!\bigl(X,\ \mathcal F\otimes \mathcal L^{\otimes n}\bigr).$$
    
2.  For every $t'\in \Gamma(X_s,\mathcal F)$ there exist $n>0$ and $t\in \Gamma\!\bigl(X,\ \mathcal F\otimes \mathcal L^{\otimes n}\bigr)$ such that
    
    $$t|_{X_s}=t'\otimes s^{\otimes n}.$$
    

_Proof._ Choose a finite affine cover $X=\bigcup_i U_i$ with trivializations $w_i:\mathcal L|_{U_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal O_{U_i}$. Write $s_i:=w_i(s|_{U_i})\in \Gamma(U_i,\mathcal O_X)$. Then $X_s\cap U_i=D(s_i)\subset U_i$.

For (1) it suffices to argue when $X$ is affine and $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_X$. Then $s$ is a global function with $X_s=D(s)$, and $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent on an affine scheme, so the localization map

$$\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)_s \longrightarrow \Gamma(D(s),\mathcal F)$$

is an isomorphism. If $t|_{D(s)}=0$, then $t/1=0$ in $\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)_s$, so there exists $n$ with $s^n t=0$ in $\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$, i.e. $t\otimes s^{\otimes n}=0$.

For (2), for each $i$ the isomorphism just used gives

$$\Gamma\!\bigl(U_i,\mathcal F\otimes \mathcal L^{\otimes n}\bigr)_{s_i}\;\cong\; \Gamma(U_i,\mathcal F)_{s_i}\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\;\Gamma(U_i\cap X_s,\mathcal F),$$

so for some $\ell>0$ (independent of $i$) the restriction $t'|_{U_i\cap X_s}$ lifts to a section

$$t_i\ \in\ \Gamma\!\bigl(U_i,\ \mathcal F\otimes \mathcal L^{\otimes \ell}\bigr).$$

On overlaps $U_i\cap U_j$ put $t_{ij}=t_i|_{U_i\cap U_j}$ and $t_{ji}=t_j|_{U_i\cap U_j}$. Then $t_{ij}-t_{ji}=0$ on $X_s\cap U_i\cap U_j$. Since $U_i\cap U_j$ is qcqs (by quasi-separatedness of $X$), part (1) applied to $U_i\cap U_j$ yields an integer $m>0$ (chosen large enough to work for all pairs $i,j$) such that

$$(t_{ij}-t_{ji})\otimes s^{\otimes m}=0\ \text{ on }U_i\cap U_j.$$

Thus the sections $t_i\otimes s^{\otimes m}$ agree on overlaps and glue to a global section

$$t\ \in\ \Gamma\!\bigl(X,\ \mathcal F\otimes \mathcal L^{\otimes(\ell+m)}\bigr)$$

with $t|_{U_i}=t_i\otimes s^{\otimes m}$. Therefore $t|_{X_s}=t'\otimes s^{\otimes(\ell+m)}$. $\square$

4\. Pullback preserves quasi-coherence (and coherence on noetherian schemes)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $f:(X,\mathcal O_X)\to(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$ be a morphism of ringed spaces. The pullback functor

$$f^*:\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathcal O_Y}\longrightarrow \operatorname{Mod}_{\mathcal O_X},\qquad f^*\mathcal G:=f^{-1}\mathcal G\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_Y}\mathcal O_X,$$

is right exact (exactness may be checked on stalks) and satisfies $f^*\mathcal O_Y\simeq \mathcal O_X$. If $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent on $Y$, then locally it is the cokernel of a map between free $\mathcal O_Y$\-modules; applying $f^*$ preserves such cokernels and sends free sheaves to free sheaves, whence $f^*\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent. If $X$ and $Y$ are noetherian schemes and $\mathcal F$ is coherent, we may choose finite-rank free presentations locally; right exactness and preservation of finite-rank free sheaves imply that $f^*\mathcal F$ is coherent.

5\. Quasi-compact morphisms and pushforward of quasi-coherent sheaves
---------------------------------------------------------------------

**Definition.** A morphism $f:X\to Y$ is **quasi-compact** if $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every affine open $V\subset Y$.

**Proposition 43.** Let $f:X\to Y$ be a qcqs morphism. If $\mathcal F$ is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$\-module, then $f_*\mathcal F$ is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_Y$\-module.

_Proof._ The assertion is local on $Y$, so assume $Y=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is affine. Then, by quasi-compactness of $f$, the scheme $X=f^{-1}(Y)$ is quasi-compact; and by quasi-separatedness of $f$, $X$ is quasi-separated. Choose a finite affine cover $X=\bigcup_i U_i$ and, for each pair $(i,j)$, a finite affine cover $U_i\cap U_j=\bigcup_k U_{ijk}$ (possible since $X$ is qcqs). The standard Čech equalizer on $X$ yields an exact sequence of sheaves

$$0 \longrightarrow \mathcal F \longrightarrow \bigoplus_i \mathcal F|_{U_i} \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{i,j,k} \mathcal F|_{U_{ijk}}.$$

Applying the left-exact functor $f_*$ and using that the index sets are finite,

$$(*)0 \longrightarrow f_*\mathcal F \longrightarrow \bigoplus_i f_*(\mathcal F|_{U_i}) \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{i,j,k} f_*(\mathcal F|_{U_{ijk}}). \tag{*}$$

Let $M:=\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)=\Gamma(Y,f_*\mathcal F)$, viewed as an $A$\-module via $A=\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)\to \Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)$. We claim that for every $g\in A$,

$$(**)\Gamma\!\bigl(D(g),\,f_*\mathcal F\bigr)\;=\;\Gamma\!\bigl(f^{-1}D(g),\,\mathcal F\bigr)\ \cong\ M_g. \tag{**}$$

Granting (\*\*), Theorem 40(iv) (applied to the affine scheme $Y$) shows that $f_*\mathcal F\simeq \widetilde{M}$, hence is quasi-coherent.

It remains to prove (\*\*). Let $s:=g\in \Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$; its pullback $f^*s\in \Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)$ is a global section of the invertible sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ with invertibility locus

$$X_{f^*s}=\{x\in X\mid f^*s\text{ is a unit at }x\}=f^{-1}D(g).$$

By Theorem 42 applied on the qcqs scheme $X$ with $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_X$ and $s=f^*g$, the natural map

$$M_s=\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)_s \longrightarrow \Gamma\!\bigl(f^{-1}D(g),\,\mathcal F\bigr)$$

is an isomorphism: injectivity follows from part (1), and surjectivity from part (2). This proves (\*\*), hence $f_*\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent. $\square$

* * *

This completes the discussion: separated morphisms are quasi-separated; the invertibility locus of a section of a line bundle is open and well behaved; on a qcqs scheme, sections over such loci satisfy a localization principle (Theorem 42), which both underlies and streamlines the proof that quasi-coherence is preserved by pullback and that pushforward along a qcqs morphism remains quasi-coherent (Proposition 43).

> The map $t\mapsto t^2$ “folds” $\mathbb A^1_{\mathbf C}$ over itself with a branch at $0$: a double cover ramified at $0$.  
> **Exercise (completed).** Over $\mathbf C$\-points, $z\mapsto z^2:\mathbf C\to\mathbf C$ is 2-to-1 on $\mathbf C^\times$ with deck involution $z\mapsto -z$, and 1-to-1 at $0$. Algebraically, on $D(u)\subset \operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C[u]$ the preimage is $D(t)\subset \operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C[t]$, and $\mathbf C[t]_t$ is a free $\mathbf C[u]_u$\-module of rank $2$ with basis $\{1,t\}$ (relation $t^2=u$), so the restriction is a finite flat double cover away from $u=0$; over $u=0$ the fiber is $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf C[t]/(t^2)$ (a single point with multiplicity), giving the indicated “X-shaped” fold.

> \[!theorem\] **Proposition 20 (Affine-locality for $A$\-algebras: finite type / f.p.)**  
> For a commutative ring $A$, the properties “$B$ is a finite-type $A$\-algebra” and “$B$ is a finitely presented $A$\-algebra” are affine-local on $\operatorname{Spec}B$. Precisely:  
> (i) If $B$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$, then $B_g$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$ for any $g\in B$.  
> (ii) If $g_1,\dots,g_n\in B$ generate the unit ideal and each $B_{g_i}$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$, then $B$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$.

**Proof (finite-type case; the f.p. case is omitted).**  
(i) If $x_1,\dots,x_r$ generate $B$ as an $A$\-algebra, then $x_1,\dots,x_r,\hat g$ generate $B_g$ over $A$.  
(ii) Write $1=\sum_i b_i g_i$. For each $i$, choose $A$\-algebra generators of $B_{g_i}$ of the form $x_{ij}/g_i^{\,m}$ (with a common exponent $m$). Let

$$R:=A\langle\, b_i g_i,\ c_{ij}\,\rangle\ \subset\ B,$$

where $c_{ij}$ are numerators with $x_{ij}=c_{ij}/g_i^{\,m}$. Then $R_{g_i}=B_{g_i}$ for all $i$. If $\mathfrak p\subset B$ is prime, some $g_i\notin\mathfrak p$; hence $(B/R)_{\mathfrak p}\cong (B/R)_{g_i}=0$. Therefore $B=R$, so $B$ is finitely generated over $A$. $\square$

> \[!proposition\] **Proposition 21 (Affine-locality on the base for finiteness conditions)**  
> Let $f:X\to S$ be a morphism of schemes. Each of the properties “finite,” “locally of finite type,” and “locally of finite presentation” is affine-local on $S$. In particular:
> 
> (a) $f$ is finite $\Longleftrightarrow$ there exists an affine cover $S=\bigcup \operatorname{Spec}A_i$ such that $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_i)$ is affine, say $\operatorname{Spec}B_i$, with $B_i$ a finite $A_i$\-module. _(Proof postponed.)_
> 
> (b) $f$ is locally of finite type (resp. locally of finite presentation) $\Longleftrightarrow$ there exists an affine cover $S=\bigcup \operatorname{Spec}A_i$ such that each $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_i)$ admits an affine open cover by spectra of finite-type (resp. finitely presented) $A_i$\-algebras.

**Proof of (b).** The “$\Rightarrow$” direction is immediate from the definition. For the converse we verify affine-locality on $S$ and then invoke Lemma 18.

(i) If $\operatorname{Spec}A\subset S$ is affine and $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\to \operatorname{Spec}A$ is locally of finite type (resp. f.p.), then for any $g\in A$,

$$f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_g)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}A_g$$

is locally of finite type (resp. f.p.): indeed, any affine $\operatorname{Spec}B\subset f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_g)$ is also an affine open of $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$, so $B$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$; then $B_g$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A_g$ by Proposition 20(i).

(ii) Suppose $g_1,\dots,g_r\in A$ generate $A$ and each

$$f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i})\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i}$$

is locally of finite type (resp. f.p.). For any affine $\operatorname{Spec}B\subset f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$, let $b_i\in B$ be the images of the $g_i$; then $(b_1,\dots,b_r)=B$ and

$$\operatorname{Spec}B_{b_i}=f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i})\cap \operatorname{Spec}B.$$

By assumption, each $B_{b_i}$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A_{g_i}$, and each $A_{g_i}$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$. By Proposition 20(ii), $B$ is finite type (resp. f.p.) over $A$. Hence $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\to \operatorname{Spec}A$ is locally of finite type (resp. f.p.). $\square$

> \[!Note\]  
> Combining Proposition 21(a) with the definition of “finite” shows: a **finite** ring map $A\to B$ induces a **finite** morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$.

16.3. Quasi-compact and finite-type morphisms
---------------------------------------------

> \[!example\] **Quasi-compact morphism (definition via an affine cover of the base)**  
> A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **quasi-compact** if there exists an open cover $S=\bigcup \operatorname{Spec}A_i$ such that each preimage $f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A_i)$ is quasi-compact.

> \[!exercise\] **Equivalent formulation (completed).**  
> Show equivalently: $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V\subset S$.  
> _Solution._ If $f$ is quasi-compact in the first sense, cover a quasi-compact $V$ by finitely many $V\cap \operatorname{Spec}A_i$; then $f^{-1}(V)=\bigcup f^{-1}(V\cap \operatorname{Spec}A_i)$ is a finite union of quasi-compact opens and hence quasi-compact. Conversely, if the condition holds for all quasi-compact $V$, apply it to each affine $V=\operatorname{Spec}A_i$ in an affine cover of $S$.

> \[!definition\] **Finite-type morphism**  
> A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **of finite type** if it is locally of finite type and quasi-compact.

> \[!example\] **Examples**
> 
> 1.  If $S=\operatorname{Spec}R$, then $f:X\to S$ is quasi-compact iff $X$ is quasi-compact.  
>     _Proof._ The preimage of the single affine open $S$ is $X$, so the two notions coincide.
>     
> 2.  Any **closed immersion** is quasi-compact and locally of finite type, hence of finite type.  
>     _Proof._ Locally on $S$, such a map is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)\to \operatorname{Spec}A$, whose source is closed in an affine (therefore quasi-compact) and whose structure map $A\to A/I$ is of finite type (quotient of a polynomial ring in zero variables), exactly matching the definition of “locally of finite type.”
>     

> \[!NOTE\]  
> A **finitely presented** morphism $X\to S$ is not merely “locally finitely presented + quasi-compact”; there is an additional condition (to be introduced later).

### Concluding remarks

The key takeaways are:

*   “Locally noetherian” is truly local on affine opens (Proposition 19).
    
*   For $A$\-algebras, “finite type” and “finitely presented” are affine-local (Proposition 20).
    
*   For morphisms, “finite / locally of finite type / locally finitely presented” are affine-local on the base (Proposition 21); in particular, a finite ring map yields a finite morphism.
    
*   “Finite type” for a morphism means “locally of finite type + quasi-compact,” with quasi-compactness characterized by quasi-compact preimages of quasi-compact opens.
    

These principles justify checking finiteness on convenient affine charts without ambiguity across different affine covers.
