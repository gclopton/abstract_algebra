## Part 1


Let $A=A_{\bullet}$ and $B=A^{(d)}$. So $B=\bigoplus_{m \geq 0} A_{d m}$, and we have

$$
X=\operatorname{Proj} A, \quad X^{\prime}=\operatorname{Proj} B .
$$


There is the standard isomorphism $X \xrightarrow{\sim} X^{\prime}$ coming from the inclusion of graded rings $B \hookrightarrow A$. Write $\varphi: X \xrightarrow{\sim} X^{\prime}$ for the isomorphism induced by $B \hookrightarrow A$.
What we must show is

$$
\varphi^* \mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(d)
$$




Recall the general dictionary: for a graded ring $C$ and a graded $C$-module $M=\bigoplus_i M_i$, the associated quasi-coherent sheaf $\widetilde{M}$ on $Y=\operatorname{Proj} C$ is defined by

$$
\widetilde{M}\left(D_{+}(f)\right)=\left(M_f\right)_0
$$

for homogeneous $f \in C_{+}$, where $\left(M_f\right)_0$ is the degree-zero part of the localized module $M_f$. In particular

$$
\mathcal{O}_Y(1)=\widetilde{C(1)}, \quad \mathcal{O}_Y(m)=\widetilde{C(m)},
$$

where $C(m)$ is the shifted module $(C(m))_i=C_{i+m}$. We want to compare $\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(1)=\widetilde{B(1)}$ on $X^{\prime}$ with $\mathcal{O}_X(d)=\widetilde{A(d)}$ on $X$, via $\varphi$. Because $A_{+}$is generated by $A_1$, the distinguished open subsets $D_{+}(f) \subset X$ with $\operatorname{deg}(f)=d$ cover $X$ , and these same elements $f \in A_d$ lie in $B_1$. Under the identification $X \cong X^{\prime}$, each such $D_{+}(f)$ is the same subset in both $X$ and $X^{\prime}$. So it suffices to compare the restrictions of the two line bundles on each $D_{+}(f)$ for homogeneous $f \in A_d$. On $X^{\prime}$, we have

$$
\left.\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(1)\right|_{D_{+}(f)} \quad \text { corresponds to } \quad(B(1))_{(f), 0}
$$

the degree zero part of the localization of the shifted module $B(1)$ at $f \in B_1=A_d$. On $X$, we have

$$
\left.\mathcal{O}_X(d)\right|_{D_{+}(f)} \quad \text { corresponds to } \quad(A(d))_{(f), 0}
$$

the degree zero part of the localization of $A(d)$ at the same element $f$ (now viewed in the bigger ring $A$ ). So the task is to show there is a natural isomorphism of modules

$$
(B(1))_{(f), 0} \cong(A(d))_{(f), 0},
$$

compatible with restriction maps, for each homogeneous $f \in A_d$. Once we have this, the local isomorphisms glue to a global isomorphism of line bundles, which is exactly the desired statement. Now compute degrees explicitly.


1. The Veronese ring is $B=A^{(d)}$ with

$$
B_m=A_{d m}
$$


In particular, for $f \in A_d$, we have $f \in B_1$.
2. The shifted module $B(1)$ is defined by $B(1)_n=B_{n+1}$. So

$$
B(1)_n=B_{n+1}=A_{d(n+1)}
$$

3. Localizing $B(1)$ at $f$ gives elements of the form

$$
\frac{s}{f^k}, \quad s \in B(1) \text { homogeneous. }
$$


In the grading of the module $B(1)$, such an element has degree

$$
\operatorname{deg}_{B(1)}\left(\frac{s}{f^k}\right)=\operatorname{deg}_{B(1)}(s)-k \operatorname{deg}_B(f)=\operatorname{deg}_{B(1)}(s)-k,
$$

because $\operatorname{deg}_B(f)=1$.
Thus $\frac{s}{f^k}$ has degree zero in $B(1)_f$ if and only if

$$
\operatorname{deg}_{B(1)}(s)=k .
$$

4. Write $\operatorname{deg}_{B(1)}(s)=n$. Then by step $2, s \in B(1)_n=A_{d(n+1)}$. If we impose $n=k$, we see that degree-zero elements of $(B(1))_{(f)}$ are precisely fractions of the form

$$
\frac{s}{f^k}, \quad s \in A_{d(k+1)}, k \geq 0
$$


Now do the same with $A(d)$.
1. The shifted module $A(d)$ is defined by $A(d)_n=A_{n+d}$.
2. Localizing at $f \in A_d$, an element of $A(d)_f$ looks like $\frac{a}{f^m}$ with $a \in A$ homogeneous. In the module grading we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \qquad \operatorname{deg}_{A(d)}\left(\frac{a}{f^m}\right)=\operatorname{deg}_{A(d)}(a)-m \operatorname{deg}_A(f)=\left(\operatorname{deg}_A(a)-d\right)-m d \text {, } \\
& \text { since } \operatorname{deg}_A(f)=d \text {. }
\end{aligned}
$$


Thus the degree-zero condition is

$$
\left(\operatorname{deg}_A(a)-d\right)-m d=0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{deg}_A(a)=(m+1) d .
$$


So degree-zero elements of $(A(d))_{(f)}$ are precisely fractions of the form

$$
\frac{a}{f^m}, \quad a \in A_{(m+1) d}, m \geq 0
$$


Comparing the two descriptions, we see they are literally describing the same subset of the localization $A_f$ : both are "fractions $\frac{\text { something }}{f^k}$ with numerator in $A_{d(k+1)}$ ".



Hence we have a canonical identification

$$
(B(1))_{(f), 0} \xrightarrow{\sim}(A(d))_{(f), 0}, \quad \frac{s}{f^k} \longmapsto \frac{s}{f^k}
$$

just viewing everything inside the same localized ring $A_f$. This is well-defined because equality of fractions in both modules is equality in $A_f$; and it is bijective because the degree condition defining both sides is identical. These isomorphisms for all distinguished opens $D_{+}(f)$ with $\operatorname{deg}(f)=d$ glue to a global isomorphism of line bundles on $X$ :

$$
\varphi^* \mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(1) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(d)
$$

as required.



## Part ii


Now specialize to the standard graded polynomial ring

$$
A=R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right], \quad \operatorname{deg}\left(x_i\right)=1 .
$$


The $d$-th Veronese subring is

$$
A^{(d)}=\bigoplus_{m \geq 0} A_{d m},
$$

where $A_{d m}$ is the $R$-module of homogeneous polynomials of degree $d m$ in the $x_i$ 's. Let $N$ be the number of monomials of degree $d$ in the variables $x_0, \ldots, x_n$. Choose an ordering $\left\{M_1, \ldots, M_N\right\}$ of these degree $-d$ monomials.

Consider the polynomial ring

$$
S=R\left[y_1, \ldots, y_N\right]
$$

with the standard grading $\operatorname{deg}\left(y_j\right)=1$ for all $j$. Define a graded $R$-algebra homomorphism

$$
\psi: S \longrightarrow A^{(d)}
$$

by sending each degree-one generator to the corresponding degree- $d$ monomial:

$$
\psi\left(y_j\right)=M_j\left(x_0, \ldots, x_n\right) \in A_d=A_1^{(d)} .
$$


This is clearly homogeneous of degree 1.



Claim: $\psi$ is surjective. It suffices to show that the degree- $d$ monomials generate $A^{(d)}$ as an $R$-algebra. Indeed, a general homogeneous element of $A^{(d)}$ in degree $m$ is a linear combination of monomials of total degree $m d$ in the $x_i$ 's. Take any monomial

$$
x_0^{a_0} \cdots x_n^{a_n} \quad \text { with } \quad a_0+\cdots+a_n=m d
$$


We can factor this monomial as a product of $m$ monomials each of total degree $d$. For example, think of placing $m d$ indistinguishable "degree units" and grouping them into $m$ blocks of size $d$ : this corresponds to writing the exponent vector $\left(a_0, \ldots, a_n\right)$ as a sum of $m$ exponent vectors of total degree $d$. Doing this combinatorially shows

$$
x_0^{a_0} \cdots x_n^{a_n}=M_{j_1} \cdots \cdots M_{j_m}
$$

for some degree- $d$ monomials $M_{j_k}$. Thus every monomial of degree $m d$ is in the sub-algebra generated by the degree- $d$ monomials, hence the image of $\psi$ is all of $A^{(d)}$. Therefore $A^{(d)} \cong S / I$ for a homogeneous ideal $I=\operatorname{ker}(\psi) \subset S$.
Now use the basic Proj-quotient correspondence: if $S$ is a graded ring and $I \subset S$ is a homogeneous ideal, then

$$
\operatorname{Proj}(S / I) \cong V_{+}(I) \subset \operatorname{Proj} S
$$

where $V_{+}(I)$ is the closed subscheme cut out by $I$ inside $\operatorname{Proj} S$.  Here $S=R\left[y_1, \ldots, y_N\right]$, so

$$
\operatorname{Proj} S=\mathbb{P}_R^{N-1}
$$


Hence

$$
\operatorname{Proj}\left(A^{(d)}\right) \cong \operatorname{Proj}(S / I) \cong V_{+}(I) \subset \operatorname{Proj} S=\mathbb{P}_R^{N-1}
$$


Thus $\operatorname{Proj}\left(A^{(d)}\right)$ is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}_R^{N-1}$, as claimed. Combining this with part (i) and the known isomorphism

$$
\mathbb{P}_R^n=\operatorname{Proj} R\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right] \xrightarrow{\sim} \operatorname{Proj} A^{(d)} \subset \mathbb{P}_R^{N-1},
$$

we obtain the $d$-uple Veronese embedding

$$
\nu_d: \mathbb{P}_R^n \hookrightarrow \mathbb{P}_R^{N-1}
$$

which on homogeneous coordinates sends

$$
\left[x_0: \cdots: x_n\right] \longmapsto\left[\cdots: M_j\left(x_0, \ldots, x_n\right): \ldots\right]_{j=1}^N,
$$

i.e. to the point whose coordinates are all degree- $d$ monomials in the $x_i$..



# Question 2


Let $k$ be a field, $X=\mathbb{P}_k^n=\operatorname{Proj} k\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]$, and let $f \in k\left[x_0, \ldots, x_n\right]$ be an irreducible homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$. Set $Y=V_{+}(f) \subset X$ and $U=D_{+}(f)=X \backslash Y$. We want to show that

$$
\mathrm{Cl}(U) \cong \mathbb{Z} / d \mathbb{Z}
$$



First, $\mathbb{P}_k^n$ is smooth, hence normal, integral, and regular. For such a scheme, Cartier and Weil divisors agree, and we have an isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \operatorname{Pic}(X) .
$$


In particular,

$$
\mathrm{Cl}\left(\mathbb{P}_k^n\right) \cong \mathbb{Z},
$$

generated by the class of a hyperplane $H$, and $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$ corresponds to that generator. Now consider the hypersurface $Y=V_{+}(f)$. Because $f$ is a single homogeneous polynomial, $Y$ is locally defined by one equation, so it is an effective Cartier divisor on $X$. Its associated invertible sheaf is

$$
\mathcal{O}_X(Y) .
$$


On the other hand, $f$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $d$, so we can view $f$ as a global section of the line bundle $\mathcal{O}_X(d)$. Its zero locus as a section of $\mathcal{O}_X(d)$ is exactly the divisor $Y$. That means the line bundle attached to the divisor $Y$ is $\mathcal{O}_X(d)$ :

$$
\mathcal{O}_X(Y) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(d) .
$$


Under the identification $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \simeq \operatorname{Pic}(X)$, the class of a divisor $D$ corresponds to the isomorphism class of $\mathcal{O}_X(D)$. Thus:


- The hyperplane class $[H]$ corresponds to $\mathcal{O}_X(1)$.
- The class $[Y]$ corresponds to $\mathcal{O}_X(Y) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(d)$.

Since $\mathcal{O}_X(d) \cong \mathcal{O}_X(1)^{\otimes d}$, we get in $\mathrm{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}[H]$ that

$$
[Y]=d[H] .
$$


So inside $\operatorname{Cl}\left(\mathbb{P}_k^n\right) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, the hypersurface $Y$ is exactly $d$ times the generator. Now we relate the class group of the open set $U=X \backslash Y$ to that of $X$.


Let $X$ be a normal integral noetherian scheme, and $U \subset X$ an open subset. Let the complement $Z= X \backslash U$ be a union of codimension-one integral subschemes. Then there is an exact sequence where each summand $\mathbb{Z}$ is generated by a prime divisor $Z_i$, and the map sends $1 \in \mathbb{Z}$ in that summand to the class $\left[Z_i\right]$ in $\mathrm{Cl}(X)$.

In our situation:
- $X=\mathbb{P}_k^n$ is normal and integral.
- $U=D_{+}(f)=X \backslash Y$.
- The complement is just the single irreducible hypersurface $Y$, which is a prime divisor of codimension one.

So the direct sum in the localization sequence is just one copy of $\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $Y$. The sequence becomes

$$
\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\alpha} \mathrm{Cl}(X) \longrightarrow \mathrm{Cl}(U) \longrightarrow 0,
$$

where $\alpha(1)=[Y]$.
We already identified $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ with generator $[H]$, and computed $[Y]=d[H]$. Under the isomorphism $\operatorname{Cl}(X) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, the map $\alpha$ is just multiplication by $d$.

Thus the exact sequence above is

$$
\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{\times d} \mathbb{Z} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Cl}(U) \longrightarrow 0 .
$$


The cokernel of $\mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{x d} \mathbb{Z}$ is precisely $\mathbb{Z} / d \mathbb{Z}$. Therefore

$$
\mathrm{Cl}(U) \cong \mathbb{Z} / d \mathbb{Z}
$$

which is exactly what we wanted to show.



# Question 3


We work in $A=k[x, y]$ and set

$$
I=\left(x y, x^2\right) \subset A, \quad X=V(I) \subset \mathbb{A}_k^2=\operatorname{Spec} A .
$$


First compute the radical of $I$. Clearly $x^2 \in I$, so any prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ containing $I$ must contain $x$ (since $x^2 \in \mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{p}$ is prime). Thus every prime over $I$ contains ( $x$ ), hence

$$
\sqrt{I} \supseteq(x) .
$$


Conversely, $I \subset(x)$, because

$$
\left(x y, x^2\right)=x \cdot(y, x) \subset(x) .
$$


So any prime containing $I$ must contain $(x)$, and since $(x)$ itself is prime, it is the unique minimal prime over $I$. That means

$$
\sqrt{I}=(x) .
$$


Topologically, $V(I)=V(\sqrt{I})=V(x)$. The closed subset $V(x) \subset \mathbb{A}_k^2$ is the vertical line $\{x=0\}$, which is irreducible because $(x)$ is a prime ideal. Hence the underlying topological space of the scheme $X$ is irreducible. Scheme-theoretically, we can think of $X$ as the line $x=0$ endowed with a non-reduced ("thickened") structure coming from the nilpotents in $A / I$. The ambient space $\mathbb{A}_k^2=\operatorname{Spec} k[x, y]$ has dimension 2. The closed subset $V(x)$ is isomorphic to Spec $k[y]$, which has dimension 1 . Thus

$$
\operatorname{dim} V(x)=1,
$$

so the codimension of $V(x)$ in $\mathbb{A}_k^2$ is

$$
\operatorname{codim}\left(V(x), \mathbb{A}_k^2\right)=2-1=1
$$



Since $X$ has the same underlying topological space as $V(x)$, it is an irreducible closed subset of codimension 1 in $\mathbb{A}_k^2$.



A closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is given by a quasi-coherent sheaf of ideals, which in the affine case is just an ideal $J \subset A$. Two ideals $J_1, J_2$ define the same closed subscheme if and only if $J_1=J_2$. Thus saying

$$
X=V(f) \text { for some } f \in A
$$

as a closed subscheme is equivalent to saying

$$
I=\left(x y, x^2\right)=(f)
$$

as ideals in $A$. Assume for contradiction that $I$ is principal and generated by some $f \in A$. Then $x^2 \in I$ and $x y \in I$ imply that $f$ divides both $x^2$ and $x y$ in the UFD $k[x, y]$. In a UFD, any common divisor of $x^2$ and $x y$ must divide their gcd, which is

$$
\operatorname{gcd}\left(x^2, x y\right)=x
$$


Hence $f$ divides $x$. Since $f$ is not a unit (the ideal $I$ is proper), it follows that $f$ is associate to $x$, i.e. $f= u x$ for some unit $u \in k^{\times}$. Therefore

$$
I=(f)=(x)
$$



But $I=\left(x^2, x y\right)$ is strictly smaller than $(x)$ : for example, $x \in(x)$ but $x \notin\left(x^2, x y\right)$. Indeed, if $x \in \left(x^2, x y\right)$, then there exist $a, b \in A$ such that

$$
x=a x^2+b x y=x(a x+b y),
$$

so in the domain $k[x, y]$ we could cancel $x$ and obtain $1=a x+b y$, i.e. $1 \in(x, y)$, which is impossible. Thus $\left(x^2, x y\right) \subsetneq(x)$, contradicting $I=(x)$. So the assumption that $I$ is principal leads to a contradiction. Therefore $I$ is not principal, and there is no $f \in k[x, y]$ such that $I=(f)$. Equivalently, the closed subscheme

$$
X=V\left(x y, x^2\right)
$$

is not equal to $V(f)$ for any single polynomial $f$.


So $X$ has irreducible underlying topological space of codimension one (it is the line $x=0$ with a non-reduced structure), but its defining ideal is not principal, so it cannot be presented as the zero locus of a single equation $f$.
